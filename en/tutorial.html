Home Assistant Onboarding Guide

From scratch to functional

Set the newly installed Home Assistant from a blank state all the way to the point where you can turn on lights with two clicks on the wall, receive notifications on your phone, and then go down to communication protocols, energy monitoring, voice assistants, and field-level network architecture. 22 chapters + 3 appendices, 10 minutes each. Sales brochures, AI prompt lexicon and skill manuals are in Resource Hub.

Foundations: from zero to working

01

Log in to Home Assistant

Understand the differences between login pages, remember me, Companion App and web pages.

 02

System settings

Correct the time zone, language, unit, and geographical location once, and all subsequent automation will be accurate.

 03

Floors and areas

First draw the floors and rooms of your home, and then the controls, dashboard, and voice will be clear.

 04

Naming and labels

Turn “switch.0x00abc123” into the “living room main light” and use labels to group similar devices.

 05

User management

Create accounts for family members, tenants, and guests, and decide who can change settings and who can only turn on lights.

 06

Dashboards

Put the most commonly used lights, curtains, and air conditioners on the homepage, and your family will use them as soon as they are turned on.

 07

Notifications

Install the Companion App, bind your phone, and send the first notification.

 08

Your first automation

The three-stage trigger/condition/action avoids the device_id trap and writes “turn on the lights at sunset”.

 09

backups

Set up automatic backup in 3 minutes, and then you can safely try out integrations and change settings.

 10

Scripts

Packing repeated actions into a button is more flexible than automation and can be called with parameters.

 11

Device settings

How to view the device page, how to upgrade the firmware, how to deactivate the entity, and how to save the repair center.

 12

Domain reference

light / climate / cover / media_player… The common actions, UI cards, and common pitfalls of each Domain will be discussed in one go.

Extensions: connect more devices

13

Themes and appearance

Dark mode, social theme, everyone has their own style, and adjust the interface to how you want to live in it.

 14

Integrations

Weather, music, positioning, calendar - only when outside services are brought in, will the home know what is happening outside.

 15

Communication protocols

Wi-Fi / Zigbee / Z-Wave / Matter / KNX / Modbus will be explained in one go. Read this chapter before buying the equipment.

 16

To-do lists and calendars

Take out the trash, pay the bill, change the filter - let the home remember what to do and remind you at the right time.

Data, energy, and AI

17

Energy dashboard and ESG practices

Turn electricity bills into understandable charts, find out the electricity-hungry monsters in your home, and make ESG reports.

 18

History, Recorder, and data export

Where does the data exist, how long it can be stored, how to download it to CSV, and why your HA is running slower and slower.

 19

Voice assistants and LLM integration

From “turning on the lights in the living room” to talking to AI to manage your home, both local and cloud approaches are available.

Advanced architecture and applications

20

Add-ons and Docker architecture

Add-on is actually a Docker container. Only after understanding this layer can you understand why HA is so long.

 21

Networking and remote access

Fixed IP, reverse proxy, Cloudflare Tunnel, VPN—connect home securely even when you’re outside.

 22

Life logging and custom dashboards

Drinking water, keeping accounts, changing filters, family message boards - turn daily chores into a system that can draw its own diagrams.

Appendices

A

Ecosystem: HACS, add-ons, and remote access

Comparison of official add-ons, community kit store, and four external connection solutions. Come back after installing the main line.

 B

Scenes, helpers, and groups

Scene, 10 types of Helper, and Group are three advanced weapons, making your home like a small program.

 C

Before installation: hardware and HAOS

Don’t have a host yet? Read this first. How to choose hardware, how to burn HAOS, and what to set when booting for the first time.
