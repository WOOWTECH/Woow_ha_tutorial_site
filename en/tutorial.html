Home Assistant Onboarding Guide

From setup to everyday use

Take a newly installed Home Assistant system from a blank slate to turning on a light with two taps on a wall panel and receiving notifications on your phone. Then continue with communication protocols, energy monitoring, voice assistants, and professional-grade network architecture. The guide has 22 chapters and 3 appendices, each designed to take about 10 minutes. Find the Sales Handbook, Prompt Library, and Skill Handbook in the Resource Hub.

Foundations: from setup to everyday use

01

Log in to Home Assistant

Get to know the sign-in screen and “Stay logged in”, and learn when to use the web interface or Companion App.

 02

System settings

Set the time zone, language, units, and location correctly from the outset so that every automation runs as expected.

 03

Floors and areas

Map your home’s floors and rooms so that controls, dashboards, and voice assistants know which device is where.

 04

Naming and labels

Turn “switch.0x00abc123” into “Living Room Main Light”, then use labels to group similar devices.

 05

User management

Create accounts for family members, tenants, and guests, deciding who can change settings and who can only control devices.

 06

Dashboards

Put your most-used lights, curtains, and air-conditioning controls on the home screen so that everyone can use them straight away.

 07

Notifications

Install the Companion App, connect a phone, and send your first push notification.

 08

Your first automation

Use the trigger-condition-action model to turn on a light at sunset without falling into the device_id trap.

 09

Backups

Set up automatic backups in three minutes, then experiment with integrations and settings with confidence.

 10

Scripts

Bundle repeated actions into a script you can run at the press of a button or call with parameters.

 11

Device settings

Learn your way around the device page, update firmware, disable entities, and troubleshoot problems with Repairs.

 12

Domain Control Reference

A practical guide to common actions, dashboard cards, and pitfalls for light, climate, cover, media_player, and other domains.

Extensions: connect more devices

13

Themes and appearance

Switch to dark mode, install community themes, and give each user a style of their own.

 14

Integrations

Connect external weather, music, location, and calendar services so that your home can respond to what is happening outside.

 15

A complete guide to communication protocols

Compare Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Matter, Thread, Bluetooth, KNX, Modbus, and MQTT before buying a device.

 16

To-do lists and calendars

Let your home remember bin day, bills, and filter changes—and remind you at the right time.

Data, energy, and AI

17

Energy Dashboard and ESG Practice

Turn electricity bills into clear charts, identify the appliances using the most power, and produce ESG reports.

 18

History, Recorder, and Data Export

Learn where Home Assistant stores data, how long it is retained, how to export it as CSV, and why the system can slow down over time.

 19

Voice assistants and LLM integration

Go from “turn on the living room light” to an AI-powered household assistant, with both local and cloud approaches explained.

Advanced architecture and applications

20

Apps, add-ons, and Docker architecture

Learn how an app—formerly called an add-on—runs as a Docker container, and understand why Home Assistant is structured this way.

 21

Networking and remote access

Set a static IP and compare reverse proxies, Cloudflare Tunnel, and VPNs for secure access while you are away from home.

 22

Life logging and custom dashboards

Track water intake, household spending, filter changes, and family messages, then turn everyday records into useful dashboards.

Appendices

A

Ecosystem: HACS, apps, and remote access

Compare official apps, the HACS community store, and four ways to connect from outside your home.

 B

Scenes, helpers, and groups

Use scenes, ten types of helper, and groups as powerful building blocks for more sophisticated automations.

 C

Before installation: hardware and HAOS

No host yet? Start here to choose your hardware, install HAOS, and complete the initial setup.
