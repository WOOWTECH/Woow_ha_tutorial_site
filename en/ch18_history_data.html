Chapter 18

Historical data, Recorder and data export

Every time you turn on the lights, every time the electricity is turned on, every time the door is opened, Home Assistant actually silently records it. This chapter walks you through where the data exists, how long it lasts, how to turn it into a CSV file and throw it into a spreadsheet, and how to slim down the database before it fills up your memory card.

Why should you learn this?

In the previous chapters, you have connected the device and started the automation. Two things will happen next, and almost certainly will:

first item: One day you want to know "Who turned on the balcony lights all night last Wednesday night", or "How much electricity did the air conditioner consume this month?" You click on it and find that the data from three days ago is still there, but the data from three months ago is gone - but the strange thing is that the electricity consumption can be seen from last year. This isn't broken, it's by design, and this chapter will explain why.

second item: One day, your Home Assistant suddenly becomes extremely slow. It takes three seconds to turn on a light and ten minutes to restart. Eighty percent of the time, the database is too fat. If you run on the Raspberry Pi's memory card and leave it alone, the next step is to directly kill the memory card and refill the entire system.

Concept:Home Assistant doesn't just have a "now", it also has a "past". Taking good care of the "past" is the watershed in evolving from a toy to a stable system.

After reading this chapter, you will have these abilities:

  • You can distinguish between "History" and "Activity (formerly known as Logbook)" which one should go to find the answer.
  • Know which file the Recorder will store the data in, how many days it will be kept by default, and how big it will probably be.
  • Will use include / exclude Kick the noisy entity out of the database.
  • You can download CSV with a few clicks from the screen and throw it into Excel or Google spreadsheets to draw.
  • Know how to clean up the database when it is fat, how to save it when it is damaged, and where to move it for long-term storage.

The general rules for backup are already in Chapter 9 As I said before, I will only add the section on "The entanglement between backup and database" here. Entity naming and entity_id Please see the rules Chapter 4, the exclusion settings in this chapter will be used extensively.

History and Activity: Two Sidebar Items, Two Questions

You will see two similar things on the sidebar:History follow Activity. Many people think they are the same, but in fact they answer completely different questions.

Warning:"Activity" was called "Logbook" before Home Assistant 2025.10. In that version, the official changed the word on the interface to Activity, but the underlying integration name and YAML setting block are still logbook. If you see the old teaching on writing "Logbook" on the Internet, this is what it is about.
Questions you want to askwhere to seewhy
"What is the curve of the living room temperature in the past three days?"historyHistory will draw numerical values into a line chart, which is suitable for looking at trends.
"How many hours were the lights in the entrance hall turned on yesterday?"historySwitch entities are color block timelines in history, and you can tell at a glance how long they occupy.
"Who turned off the air conditioner just now?"ActivitiesThe activity will write "who/which automation triggered it"
"What happened at three o'clock in the morning?"ActivitiesActivities are a sequence of events in reverse order, scrolling down from the newest to the oldest.
"Did this automation really work?"ActivitiesAutomation being triggered will leave a markup on the activity

In a life-oriented way:The history is the graph of the physical examination report, and the activity is the shift record of the nursing station.The former tells you "how the numbers changed", and the latter tells you "who did what".

There is another practically important difference:Active defaults will automatically skip sensors with units. Temperature, humidity, and power, which have numbers that jump every few seconds, if all were thrown into the event, the screen would be washed out and completely unreadable, so the official directly excluded "sensors with units" from the event. If you want to see them, go to history.

Tip:Both pages share the same source (logger). So if you exclude an entity from the recorder, it will disappear both in history and activity, not just on one side.

Recorder: the source of all history

History and activities themselves do not store data, they are just "displays". What actually writes every state change to the hard disk behind the scenes is called Recorder integration.

You didn't install it because it was on in the first place. The official documentation is very clear: historical integration relies on a recorder to store data, and both sides share the same database settings; and as long as your configuration.yaml That line is there default_config:(It comes by default during a new installation), these things will be enabled automatically. So the answer to "I never set it, why is there history?" is this line.

Which file does the data exist in?

By default, Home Assistant uses SQLite, the database is a file in the settings folder:

/config/home-assistant_v2.db

Just such a file, all your history is in it. it follows configuration.yaml If they are stored in the same folder, they will be included in the complete backup (the trouble of this matter will be discussed later).

Preset how many days to stay

The logger has a function called purge_keep_days settings,Default is 10 days. In other words, "transaction status changes" that exceed 10 days will be automatically deleted.

It is accompanied by two switches that are turned on by default:

OptionsDefault valuewhat is it doing
purge_keep_days10How many days to keep transaction history
auto_purgetrueExpired data is automatically cleared every early morning. The execution time written in the official document is 04:12 local time.
auto_repacktrueEvery other Sunday, after cleaning, reorganize the database and return the space to the hard drive.
commit_interval5Write accumulated changes to the database every few seconds
Concept:commit_interval It's a very interesting design. If you write to the hard disk every time there is a change, the memory card will be destroyed quickly; so Home Assistant first saves it in the memory for 5 seconds and then writes it again. Increasing it (for example, 30) can significantly reduce the number of writes, at the cost of losing at most the data for that period of time in the event of a power outage.

How big will the database be?

There's no standard answer, as it all depends on how many entities you have, and how noisy those entities are. But I can give you a way to get a feel for it:

situationApproximate scalereasonable expectations
Just moved in, a few lights and a thermometer and hygrometer20 to 50 entitiesDozens of MB levels, no worries at all
An average family, with doors, windows, a human body, and a few appliances added100~300 entitiesHundreds of MB are the norm
Installed smart meter/solar energy/second-by-second power monitoringContains high frequency numerical sensorMay reach several gigabytes and grow quickly

The key is not "a few entities", but "How many writes does each entity write per day?”. A lamp may only change 6 times a day; a power socket that reports second by second can write tens of thousands of entries a day. A single such outlet is enough to make the database larger than everything else combined.

If you want to know how old you are now, official documents give two ways:

What you want to seehow to gosee what
How many disks are left on the entire machine?Settings → System → StorageMove your mouse over the status bar to display details of the remaining space.
How big is the database itself?Settings → System → Repairs → Three dots in the upper right corner → System InformationScroll down to find the Recorder area, where there is "Estimated database size (Estimated database size)", the unit is MiB

The later "SQL Integration" section will also teach you to make this number into a sensor, pin it directly on the dashboard and stare at it, without having to turn to the system information every time.

Warning:The positions and field names of these pages will be slightly adjusted in different versions, please refer to what you see on your own screen. If you can't find it, just type "save" or "system" in the search box at the top of the settings page.

Long-term statistics: Why can I see last year’s electricity bills but not last year’s switches?

This is the most confusing and most understandable section of the entire chapter.

I just said that the default setting is only 10 days. But when you open the energy dashboard, the electricity consumption from last year is clearly still there. This isn't a bug, it's because Home Assistant is also presenttwo completely different things.

Short term history (status)Long-term Statistics
What to saveEvery status change, no one is missedSummed up into one sum per hour
ResolutionAuthentic flavor, at every hour, minute and secondHourly minimum/maximum/average, or cumulative amount for that hour
How long to keepDefault 10 days, delete after thatwill not be cleared
who would haveAll entities that are not excludedOnly entities with state class
How many transactions a dayLook how noisy it is, maybe tens of thousands of dollarsFixed 24 pens

The official information document makes it very straightforward: the status table "records what happened as it is", and statistics compresses the data into summaries at fixed intervals, so only 24 transactions are generated a day, and it can be stored for several years with a little space.

Which entities will have long-term statistics?

Only with "status category (state_class)" will be entered into the statistics table. What do the three status categories represent and how to set them?Chapter 17 It has been compiled into a table and explained in detail. Here I will only talk about the aspect related to "how long to stay":

status categoryWhat can be saved in statistics for you?Example at home
measurementHourly minimum, maximum, averageLiving room temperature, room humidity, and current power of air conditioner
total / total_increasingHow much did it increase during that period?The accumulated degree of the electric meter and the accumulated tonnage of the water meter
No status categoryNothing is saved, only the transaction records of the past 10 daysTurning on/off lights, opening/closing doors and windows, whether people are at home or not

So the answer is:The meter has total_increasing, so last year's electricity consumption is alive; the "on/off" of a lamp is not a numerical value and has no status category, so it only lives for 10 days.

Concept:The system actually takes a snapshot of "short-term statistics" every 5 minutes and then compiles them into hourly long-term statistics. The short-term share will be cleared regularly as in the normal state, and the long-term share will be the hourly share.

This also explains another commonly misunderstood phenomenon:When the history page goes back in time very far, the curve will suddenly become flat and blunt, and the peaks that should be there will disappear.The official document makes it very clear - the raw data with full resolution is read from the recorder in the last 10 days, and the part beyond 10 days is changed from long-term statistics, and the long-term statistics is an hourly sampling average. So the number you see will not be exactly the same as the actual reading at that moment in time, which is normal. The official even made a distinction on the picture: the lines with higher resolution will be darker.

Tip:If there is a certain value that you "will definitely want to see for several years in the future", such as a homemade water meter or gas meter, then the point is not to purge_keep_days Elongate, but confirm that entity has the correct status category and unit. With statistics, data beyond 10 days will automatically be retained.

DIY: Download history to CSV

Home Assistant has built-in ability to export history to CSV, no need to install anything. The steps are as follows.

  1. Open "History" in the sidebar

    If you can't see the sidebar, click your name at the bottom of the sidebar to enter the profile page and confirm whether it is hidden; or the fastest way - directly follow the address bar /history press Enter.

  2. Select the area, device or entity you want

    There is a selector at the top of the page. You can click on area (Area), click on device (Device), or you can directly name the entity. It's convenient to select an entire area at once - selecting the entire "living room" package, for example, will pull out the curves of everything in the living room. Provided you are here Chapter 3 There are good divisions into regions.

  3. Set time range

    Use the time range selector to select "Today", "This Week" or customize the start and end dates.This step determines how many columns there are in the CSVIf the range is too large, it will take a long time. It is recommended to test the water temperature for a day.

  4. Click "Download data" in the upper right corner

    The button is in the upper right corner of the page, and the English interface says Download data, the Chinese interface has words like "download data". Click on the browser and it will save a CSV file directly to your download folder without any setting window popping up.

  5. throw in spreadsheet

    Open in Excel, Numbers, or Google Spreads. If the Chinese becomes garbled, use "Import" instead of double-clicking to open it directly, and select UTF-8 for encoding.

Warning:The downloaded content is the same as what you see on the screen, so it is also subject to the "only hourly statistics outside 10 days" limit. You can't use this button to get the second-by-second data from three months ago - that data has been cleared long ago and no longer exists.
Tip:If you are doing homework or reporting, this method is very useful: select a temperature sensor, capture the range for a week, download the CSV, and paste it into a spreadsheet to draw a "weekly room temperature change graph." If you want to grasp the long-term trend, choose a numerical sensor with statistics. If you extend the time for one year, you will get an hourly average, which will look better when drawn.

The event page can also be downloaded

In the past, if I wanted to export a log of events such as "who did what at what time," I had to copy it myself. After Home Assistant 2026.8,The Activity page also has CSV downloads and a "Clear and Reset" action.. So now both sides have their own remittances:

The information you wantWhere to downloadsomething to get

Warning:The official release notes only mention that the activity page has a "Clear and Reset" action, but do not elaborate on what it clears. Be sure to read the confirmation prompt on the screen before pressing it. When thinking about the database, please give priority to using what is discussed later in this chapter. recorder.purge, that way you will know exactly what you have deleted.

Tools → Statistics: Fix those skewed numbers

Enter from the sidebar Settings → Tools, there are several pages in it: YAML, States, Actions, Template, Events,Statistics, Assist.

Warning:This page has had its name and location changed twice. It was originally called "Developer tools" and was hung directly on the sidebar; Home Assistant 2026.2 moved it intoSettingsBelow, 2026.8 shortened the name toTools. So when the old instructions on the Internet tell you to "click on the Developer Tools in the sidebar", now you have to go to Settings → Tools. The content is exactly the same, just moved and changed the name.

The Statistics page only does two things, but both are critical.

1. It will list problematic statistical entities

The page will list all entities with "long-term statistics" and mark the "Fix issue" links on the ones with problems. Clicking on them will tell you what problems were detected. The two most common ones are:

Symptomsvernacular explanationHow to usually deal with it

2. It can directly change the value of a certain hour

This is a lifesaver. The most typical disaster scenario: the smart meter reports an outrageous number at a certain moment (for example, it jumps to 999999 degrees instantly).Chapter 17The long bar graph of the entire month on the energy dashboard was pressed into a line close to the ground by the pillar that exploded, and nothing could be seen.

The operation described in the official document is: click the icon next to the entity on the statistics page, use the date and time fields to find the data point, and then change the value.

  1. First find the point in time when the problem occurred on an energy or historical graph

    Move your mouse over the unreasonable pillar and write down the date and hour.

  2. Go to Settings → Tools → Statistics and find the entity

    It is faster to type in the entity name using the search box above.

  3. Open the adjustment interface and use the date and time to locate the stroke

    It displays the value recorded for that hour.

  4. Change it to a reasonable value, save it and go back to look at the picture.

    Charts often require a page refresh to reflect.

Danger:What is changed here is the historical record that has been written into the database. Once changed, you cannot go back and there will be no "Restore" button. Make a backup before proceeding (Chapter 9). In addition, a single change to the cumulative sensor may affect the subsequent calculation results. After the change, be sure to go back and confirm whether the entire curve is reasonable.

Use include/exclude to streamline information

The culprit of an enlarged database is usually not the few lights you use every day, but a bunch of noise that you never look back on. To lose weight, the first thing to do is to tell the recorder to stop recording them.

This set of settings should be written in configuration.yaml, you need to restart Home Assistant after the changes are made to take effect.

Method A: Blacklist (exclude) - recommended for novices

The default is to remember all of them and only kick out the noisy ones. The mental burden is small and you won’t accidentally miss important things.

recorder:
  purge_keep_days: 10
  exclude:
    domains:
      - automation
      - update
    entity_globs:
      - sensor.sun*
      - weather.*
    entities:
      - sensor.date
      - sensor.last_boot
    event_types:
      - my_custom_event

Method B: Whitelist (include) - Used when the database is out of control

The other way around: By default, nothing will be remembered, only those you named. The effect is the most capable, but it is easy to turn off important things all together, and then realize "Hey, the history I want is gone."

recorder:
  include:
    domains:
      - sensor
      - switch
      - media_player
  exclude:
    entities:
      - sensor.last_boot
      - sensor.date
    entity_globs:
      - sensor.weather_*

The two can be mixed: use first include Circle a large area and then use exclude Pick out the few naughty bits inside. The official document lists the complete priority rules. The general principle isThe more precise the setting, the higher the priority. —Explicitly written in entities beats the wildcard, and the wildcard beats the entire domain.

Four filter conditions

ConditionsScopeWriting examples
domainsentire category- automation
entitiesDesignate a single entity- sensor.last_boot
entity_globsUniversal character batch comparison- sensor.phone_*
event_typesEvent (not entity)- call_service

What are the common noisy ghosts?

Typewhy noisyShould we exclude
The battery level, direction, and number of steps returned by the mobile appIt's almost constantly changing, and you don't go back to see itStrongly recommended to exclude
Second-by-second reporting of power socketsA single entity can take up most of the databaseDepending on the demand, you can use it instead and only keep it for a few days.

Warning:The exclusion setting only affects whether "from now on" should be included.We will not go back and delete old data that already exists in the database.. To clear out the old one, you have to use the one in the next section. recorder.purge, and to apply_filter Open.
Danger:Excluding an entity means that it will be disconnected from the history, activity, and energy dashboards, and even long-term statistics will not be generated. Energy related metersNever rule it out, after the electricity is discharged, the electricity consumption during that period will never be replenished.

Purge: Take out the trash that has been stored in it

Setting up filtering can only stop the bleeding, and the database that has grown fat still has to be cleaned manually. The recorder provides these actions (Action, formerly known as Service/Service):

actionwhat to do
recorder.purgeClear old data older than a specified number of days
recorder.purge_entitiesOnly clear specified entities/categories/wildcards
recorder.disablePause recording
recorder.enablerestore record
recorder.get_statisticsRead out long-term statistics

Three parameters of recorder.purge

parametersDefaultmeaning
keep_daysInherit values from profileCounting back from today, how many days will we need to stay?
repackfalseRewrite the entire database and really return the space to the hard disk
apply_filterfalseApply to old data together with include / exclude in the configuration file

The most commonly used "spring cleaning" combination: Just after adding the exclusion rules in the previous section and restarting the computer, run this once to clear out the debris from the past.

action: recorder.purge
data:
  keep_days: 7
  repack: true
  apply_filter: true

Execution method:Settings → Tools → Actions, find recorder.purge, switch to YAML mode, paste it, and press execute. (The old version of this page was called "Developer Tools" and was hung on the sidebar. It will be moved to the settings after 2026.2.)

Warning:Official statement repack It is a very resource-intensive operation, and the system may slow down significantly during execution, andThe process will temporarily occupy more disk space.. The documentation recommends keeping at least as much free space as the database at all times. If you run repack when the hard disk is almost full, you may make things worse - delete the old backup first to make space.

Only clear a certain entity that is noisy

If only one power socket is exploding, there is no need to bury the entire history with it.recorder.purge_entities Can be specified entity_id, domains or entity_globs, among which keep_days The default is 0, which means "all matching data will be deleted immediately."

There is a very practical automation example in the official documents: only the data of the socket for the last 5 days will be retained every morning.

alias: Clear old data from noisy power sensors every day
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "04:15:00"
actions:
  - action: recorder.purge_entities
    data:
      keep_days: 5
      entity_id: sensor.power_sensor_0
mode: single

The advantage of this way of writing is that you retain the ability to see the curve in recent days without letting it accumulate infinitely. And because long-term statistics are not affected, if this sensor has a status category, the annual trend can still be seen.

Tip:Administrator rights are required to perform actions. If you are using Chapter 5 If you create a normal user login, you will not be able to find these actions. Just switch back to the administrator account.

In addition, the preset automatic cleaning (auto_purge) will run at 04:12 every morning, soNormally you don’t need to purge manually at all. Manual operation is only used in two situations: you have just changed the filtering rules and want to apply them to old data, or the database is out of control and you need to urgently slim down.

Does the backup include the database? How to save the database if it is broken?

The backup will take away the database as well.

because home-assistant_v2.db Just lying in the settings folder, the full backup will naturally include it together. This brings a very real side effect:The size of the database is the same as the size of each backup.The database is 2 GB. If you save three backups, 6 GB will disappear out of thin air.

The official suggestion of "Clear up storage" is actually very simple, just three things: purge the database, use the recorder's filter to remember less, and purge_keep_days Turn it down. The backup side tells you to set the rules for how many copies to keep. Don't let the backup files pile up on the machine, and try to save the backups in a place other than Home Assistant.

Warning:The built-in backup interface allows you to check whether to include items such as media and shared folders. However, the status of the option "Exclude the database from backup alone" is different in different versions. Please refer to the actual check box that appears on your own screen. Rather than messing around with backup settings, it's better to slim down the database itself - that's something that works no matter which version.

It is recommended to do this in order:Set exclude first → Restart → Run once recorder.purge With repack → create a backup again.This way the backup file will be much smaller.

Changing the database is equivalent to giving up the old history

Danger:The official document states in black and white: Changing the database used by the recorder may lead to the disappearance of all existing history, andOfficially does not support data migration. So switching from SQLite to MariaDB is equivalent to "history starts anew from today." If you want to change, change it as soon as possible. Don’t wait until it accumulates for two years.

Symptoms and treatment of database corruption

The most common reasons for SQLite corruption are "power outage mid-write" or "memory card aging". The symptoms you'll usually see are: Home Assistant can be opened, but the history is empty, the activities are empty, and there are a bunch of database-related errors in the logs.

  1. First confirm whether it is really broken.

    go Settings → System → Log See if there is any sqlite or database Related error messages.

  2. Know that Home Assistant will save itself

    Official document description: When SQLite encounters irreparable damage, the system will automatically move the damaged database aside and open a brand new one. Your settings, automations, devicesWon't disappear, all that is missing is history.

  3. Make sure there is enough disk space

    Officials specifically mentioned that when dealing with a damaged SQLite database, it is necessary to prepare free space about 2.5 times the size of the database. If there is not enough space, even self-rescue cannot be achieved.

  4. Accept historical losses and find root causes

    Instead of spending hours rescuing that .db file (the official only points to the official recovery instructions of SQLite), it is better to delete the old file to make space and deal with the real problem: Is it time to replace the memory card? Do you often unplug it directly?

Tip:A broken database will almost never make your home "unusable"; the lights will still turn on and the automation will still run. It just clears the memory. So there’s really no need to panic.

Advanced 1: Switch from SQLite to MariaDB

SQLite is a single-file database, simple and requires zero settings, but it can become difficult when there are a large number of entities and frequent writing. At this time, you can switch to a regular database server.

The official document lists the minimum supported version:

DatabaseMinimum versionSuitable for whom
SQLite3.40.1By default, most people don’t need to touch it
MariaDB10.3The most common upgrade route is to install it on the same machine
MySQL8.0If you already have a MySQL server at home
PostgreSQL12People who prefer PostgreSQL

If you are running Home Assistant OS, the easiest route is to install the MariaDB App from the official repository. The path is Settings → Apps —That's right, Home Assistant 2026.2 has completely renamed the "Add-ons" on the interface to "Apps". The official statement is "Everyone knows what an app is, just open the mobile store, pick one and install it." This is what the "Add-on store" in the old teaching is about. (By the way, Apps are only available through the Home Assistant OS installation method.) After installation, configuration.yaml plus:

recorder:
  db_url: mysql://homeassistant:your password@core-mariadb/homeassistant?charset=utf8mb4
  purge_keep_days: 14

Among them core-mariadb It is the host name of that App on the internal network. The account password and database name must be consistent with what you filled in the App settings. If connecting to another machine, the general format given by the official document is:

mysql://user:password@SERVER_IP/DB_NAME?charset=utf8mb4
postgresql://user:password@SERVER_IP/DB_NAME
Warning:The database password will be written in clear text in configuration.yaml , and the file will be included in the full backup. A better practice is to create one in the same folder secrets.yaml, written inside mariadb_url: mysql://homeassistant:real password @core-mariadb/homeassistant?charset=utf8mb4, just write in the configuration file db_url: !secret mariadb_url. In this way, when posting the configuration file to the forum for help, the password will not be posted together.
Concept:Switching to MariaDB mainly solves "writing performance" and "query speed under large data volumes", but it will not automatically help you retain longer history— purge_keep_days Should it be set for a few days or a few days? If your purpose is to "keep three years of data", switching to MariaDB is not the answer, but InfluxDB in the next section is.

Advanced 2: InfluxDB + Grafana long-term storage and drawing

This is the "play it real" route. The concept is simple:

roleResponsible for whatMetaphor
Home AssistantControl your home and generate datacommand center
InfluxDBDedicated to storing time series data, it can be stored for several years without running out of timewarehouse
GrafanaDraw the data in the warehouse into a beautiful dashboardShowroom

Home Assistant officially has InfluxDB integration, which will change the statusExtraWrite another copy to InfluxDB. Note that it is "extra" - the local recorder still runs, and both sides are independent. So you can stay light with only 7 days on the recorder side, while staying 3 years on InfluxDB.

Key points in setting:

  • InfluxDB 1.x uses the combination of URL, account password, and database name, and authentication is optional (not enabled by default).
  • InfluxDB 2.x and 3.x use organization, bucket and access token instead. Verification is required, and you need to write one more in the settings. api_version: 2 Tell the integration "I am the new version". The official integration files are currently written for 1.x/2.x/3.x.
  • No matter which version,Must set filter. It also supports domains, entities, entity_globs The include / exclude writing method. Pour everything at home into InfluxDB indiscriminately, just changing the hypertrophy problem to happen in another place.
Warning:The setting parameters between the major versions of InfluxDB are quite different, and even the query language has been changed. Eighty percent of online teaching is written in an old version, so it’s normal that it won’t move when you paste it. Before starting, be sure to confirm which version you are installing, and then copy the parameter table corresponding to that version in the official integration file.

In terms of installation, InfluxDB and Grafana both have community-maintained apps (hassio-addons The "Home Assistant Community Apps" repository, formerly known as Community Add-ons),Not officially produced, you must first add that community repository to the App Store before installation. How to add a third-party warehouse Chapter 20 and Appendix A I have already mentioned it and will not repeat it here. Another reminder: App (originally called Add-on) is only available for Home Assistant OS installation. Those who install it with Docker or Core need to install InfluxDB and Grafana themselves.

Tip:The threshold for this route is not low and involves the settings of three sets of software. If you just want to see last year's electricity usage, the built-in long-term statistics plus energy dashboard will suffice. The situations where InfluxDB is really needed are usually: you want to make very customized charts, you want to mix them with non-Home Assistant data sources, or you want to retain high-resolution raw data for several years.

Advanced 3: Use SQL integration to turn queries into sensors

SQL integration allows you to write a SQL query and turn the result into an entity, which can then be placed on a dashboard and used to trigger automation. The official documentation states that it can be added through the configuration process (UI) or written in YAML.

There is only one most classic use:Make the database size a sensor and display it directly on the dashboard.

  1. Settings → Devices & Services → Add integration

    button in the lower right corner and search for "SQL".

  2. Database URL left blank

    Official documents say db_url It is optional, leaving it blank will use the database currently used by the logger. You don't need to know the path.

  3. Paste the query statement

    The official example given by the SQLite version is this sentence:

    SELECT ROUND(page_count * page_size / 1024 / 1024, 1) as size
FROM pragma_page_count(), pragma_page_size();

  4. Fill in the field size

    In the corresponding query as size That name. The query must return only one column of results.

  5. Set name and unit

    The name can be "database size", and the unit of the result is MiB. The official mentioned that you can set the device type to Data size and let the interface automatically convert units for you.

When you're done, throw it on the dashboard (Chapter 6), you can even set up an automation: when it exceeds a certain threshold, push notifications will be sent to you (Chapter 7).

alias: Notify me if the database is too big
triggers:
  - trigger: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.database_size
    above: 2000
    for: "01:00:00"
conditions: []
actions:
  - action: notify.persistent_notification
    data:
      title: Database warning
      message: The database has exceeded 2000 MiB (approximately 2 GB), it's time to find out who is spamming it.
mode: single

Used above notify.persistent_notification It will only pop up a permanent notification in the Home Assistant interface. There is no need to set anything first, so it is most suitable for testing. If you want to push it to the mobile phone, replace that line with Chapter 7 Just create the notification action.for: "01:00:00" It means "notify until it lasts for more than one hour" to prevent it from shaking around the threshold and disturbing you.

Warning:The query above is for SQLite. If you have switched to MariaDB or PostgreSQL and the syntax is completely different, go to the corresponding database file to find the equivalent query, do not copy it.
Danger:SQL integration is to issue commands directly to the database. Just run SELECT Just query, never write here DELETE, UPDATE or DROP. If you want to delete data, please use it obediently. recorder.purge, that is the formal path with security checks.

Common stuck levels

  1. Home Assistant is so slow that I have to wait for everything I order.

    Doubt the database first. Go to Settings → System → Storage to see how much disk space is left, then go to the Repair page to see the estimated database size. If it is several GB level, follow this order: first find the noisy source (usually the sensor of the mobile app, the power socket reporting every second), and add exclude, restart, and then run again recorder.purge bring repack: true and apply_filter: true. Remember to make sure there is enough disk space before running repack.

  2. The memory card is broken and the system cannot be started.

    Home Assistant every commit_interval The hard disk is written once every second, and the database is continuously long, which is quite a heavy burden on the consumer-grade microSD card. There is no official specification number to quote for this matter, but it is recognized in the community as one of the most common ways to die. There are three pragmatic approaches, with effects ranging from weak to strong: commit_interval Turn it up, cut out the noisy entities, and most importantly - boot from an SSD or industrial-grade storage instead. If you really want to run stably for a long time, replacing the memory card is the only way to cure it.

  3. After rebooting, all the history is gone.

    Two possibilities. The first is that the database is damaged. Home Assistant automatically removes the damaged one and opens a new blank database. At this time, there will be obvious error messages in the log to prove it. The second is that you have just switched from SQLite to MariaDB - data migration is not officially supported. If you switch, you will have to start over. This is expected behavior and not a fault. You can tell which one it is by looking at the log first.

  4. An entity cannot be found in history at all

    Check in order: (1) Whether it is exclude dropped, or was include The whitelist is missing; (2) Is it in Chapter 11 The place mentioned is disabled; (3) If you can't see it in "Activity" but can see it in history, that's normal - the sensor with the unit will be automatically skipped by the activity.

  5. The cleanup is finished, but the hard disk space has not increased.

    This is normal. The official document makes it very clear: purge will not return the space to the operating system immediately. repack Only then will the file be actually rewritten to free up space. And the default auto_repack It only runs every other Sunday, so the space will return on its own after a few days. If you are in a hurry, run it manually repack: true of cleaning.

  6. The energy chart was flattened by an exploding pillar

    Go to Settings → Tools → Statistics, find the entity, use the date and time to locate the outrageous value and change it. Make a backup before making any changes. For detailed steps, see the "Tools → Statistics" section of this chapter.

  7. The downloaded CSV is garbled when opened.

    Do not open directly by double-clicking. In Excel, use "Data → From Text/CSV" to import, and select UTF-8 for encoding; in Google Spreadsheet, use "File → Import", which usually automatically determines the correctness.

  8. The recorder action cannot be found, or there is no response after pressing it.

    First make sure you are on the right track: now Settings → Tools → Actions, not the "Developer Tools" on the sidebar (that location no longer exists after 2026.2). Furthermore, the actions of the recorder require administrator privileges to be executed. When logging in with a general user account, the entire setting area cannot be entered. Switch back to the administrator account and try again.

FAQ

By changing purge_keep_days to 365, will all history be saved permanently?
Technically possible, but practically not recommended. The growth of transaction-by-transaction status data is linear. Leaving it for 365 days means that the database becomes 36 times larger, queries will become slower and slower, backups will become too large to be managed, and the life of the memory card will also be accelerated.

The correct idea is to handle them separately:Numerical data rely on long-term statistics(Entities with status categories automatically leave hourly summaries and are not purged),Those who need high-resolution long-term data will be sent to InfluxDB separately..purge_keep_days Just decide "how detailed of recent data I want to see." For most people, 7 to 14 days is enough.
Why does my temperature curve three months ago look smooth and different from what I saw then?
Because that's not the source material anymore. The official document is very clear: the full resolution is read from the recorder in the last 10 days, and the excess is read from long-term statistics, and the long-term statistics is an hourly sampling average. So spikes and momentary jitters are averaged out, and the curve naturally becomes smoother.

This is not an error, but a deliberate trade-off - trading smoothness for "storing several years in a very small space." If you really need minute-by-minute data from three months ago, you can only rely on external long-term storage like InfluxDB, and the built-in mechanism cannot do it.
I added an entity to exclude, why is the old data still there?
Because the filtering rules only care about "whether you want to write from now on" and will not go back and delete things. There are two ways to clear existing old data:

One is execution recorder.purge and put apply_filter Set to true, it will apply the filtering rules in the profile to the old data. The second is to use recorder.purge_entities Just name that one entity_id, its keep_days The default is 0, which means that all data that meets the conditions will be deleted immediately.

Please note that after changing the profile, you must restart Home Assistant first for the new filtering rules to take effect.apply_filter Only then can the correct rules be applied.
After switching to MariaDB, will the previous history be transferred with it?
No. The official document clearly states: Changing the database used by the recorder may cause the existing history to disappear, andOfficially does not support data migration. Someone on the Internet has written a homemade relocation script, but that is an act at your own risk, and no one will be responsible for anything that happens.

So the decision principle is simple:If you want to change, change it as soon as possible.If you replace it just a week or two after installation, you will only lose a few days of history; if you replace it after two years of accumulation, you will lose two years of memory. If you have accumulated it for a long time and really need to change it, at least export the important parts to CSV using the "Download Data" on the history page and save it.
I just want to pour the data from a certain sensor into Excel to do my homework. What is the fastest way?
"History" in the sidebar → select the entity → set the time range → "Download data" in the upper right corner → get the CSV. The entire process does not require installing anything or writing any programs.

Two small reminders: Don’t expand the range too much at once. Use a day to test how long the file is before making a decision; also remember that what you get beyond 10 days will be the hourly average, not the original tick-by-tick data. If you want to make "detailed changes within a day", choose the last few days. If you want to make "long-term trend", just stretch it. Anyway, what you get is the summary value.
The backup files are getting bigger and bigger. Is it because of the database? Can I back up only the settings but not the history?
Yes, the database file is in the settings folder, and a full backup will include it. The official advice on backups on the "Clear Space" page is: set rules for how many copies to keep to prevent backups from accumulating, and try to save backups in a place other than Home Assistant.

The built-in backup interface allows you to choose whether to include media, shared folders and other items. However, the status of the "Exclude databases individually" option is different in different versions. Please refer to the actual check box that appears on your screen.

With or without that option, the most effective approach is the same:Let the database shrink first, and the backup will naturally be smaller.The sequence is to set exclude → reboot → run purge with repack → then create a backup.
Can "History" and "Activities" be removed from the sidebar? I can't use it.
Existing items on the sidebar can be individually hidden or reordered by long-pressing (on a mobile phone) or pressing and holding on the sidebar to enter edit mode. This setting follows the user account. You can hide it without affecting other people at home. The operation details of different versions are slightly different, please refer to the prompts on your screen.

By the way, this editing mode can only organize "things already on the sidebar", and there is no way to pull the tools (Tools) moved into the settings in 2026.2 back to the sidebar - the official said that complete sidebar customization is still under planning.

But one thing to note:Hiding the sidebar items is just out of sight, but the data is still being recorded., how big or how big should the database be? If you want to save space, you have to rely on exclude and purge in this chapter. Hiding the sidebar does not help at all. And when something really happens and you need to find out "who opened the door last night", you will miss them very much.
How do I know which entity is flooding the database?
The most intuitive way to judge:Go to the history page and see who has the densest curves one by one.High update frequency and continuous changes in values must be the main suspect. Usually, these categories cannot be escaped - the power and position returned by the mobile app, the power/current socket reported second by second, the sun azimuth angle, and the network speed test.

If you want more accurate figures, the community has various SQL queries that directly count the number of entities in the database, which can be run with the SQL integration in this chapter. However, those query writing methods will become invalid as the database structure is adjusted. The old syntax found on the Internet may not still be usable. If you are not sure, you will fall back to the crude but always effective method of "seeing who has the densest curves."
