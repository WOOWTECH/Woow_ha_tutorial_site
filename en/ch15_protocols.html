Chapter 15

Communication protocols

Compare Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, Matter, Bluetooth, KNX, Modbus, and MQTT before you buy devices. You will leave with a practical protocol map and a pre-purchase checklist.

Why should you learn this?

you are here Chapter 14 Learned how to add "Integration" in Home Assistant. But integration is a matter of the software layer. This chapter goes a step further: How does the signal fly from your phone to that light bulb?

Why bother with such low-level things? Three painful reasons:

  • Buying wrong means buying in vain. A cheap "smart switch" sold online says it supports voice assistants, but when you buy it home you find that it only recognizes your own app's cloud account, and Home Assistant can't capture it at all. Money is thrown into the water.
  • If the Internet is disconnected, the whole family will be paralyzed. Some protocols are "local control", and the lights will still turn on even if the router is disconnected; some are "cloud control", and your entrance will turn completely black when Chunghwa Telecom has a draft.
  • It was only after installing the 20th device that I discovered there was an upper limit on the number of users. Typical scenario: The whole family buys Wi-Fi sockets, and the router starts to drop out when the tenth socket is installed, because the number of devices that a home router can connect to at the same time is limited. This pitfall should be avoided before buying your first one.
Concept: Protocol is "the language used to speak between devices + the method used to transmit the sound." Choosing a protocol is choosing your home’s communication infrastructure, which is the same level of decision as choosing whether to build a house or not.

After reading this chapter, you will have the ability to do these three things: judge whether it can be used in Home Assistant when you see a product page, choose a main protocol for your home without regrets, and know which level to start checking when encountering "device offline".

What does the agreement deal with: split it into three layers

Most of the confusion comes from talking about three different things together. Take it apart first and everything will be clear from the back. Imagine you are in the living room talking to your family in the kitchen:

The first layer: radio (what is used to send the sound out)

It means "by shouting, using a walkie-talkie, or using a telephone line." Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Thread, and Bluetooth all rely on radios in the 2.4GHz frequency band; Z-Wave uses another lower frequency band; KNX and Modbus RTU do not use radios at all and use physical lines.

Second level: data format (which language is spoken)

Both of them used walkie-talkies, but one spoke Spanish and the other spoke French, so they still couldn't communicate. Zigbee devices understand each other, but Zigbee does not automatically understand Z-Wave. The reason why Matter is touted is because it wants to be the "common language that everyone can understand."

Layer 3: How does Home Assistant hear it (who can translate it for you)

Your Home Assistant console itself only has a network port and Wi-Fi, it doesn't have a Zigbee antenna or a Z-Wave antenna. So either plug in a USB receiver (Coordinator), or rely on the original gateway (Gateway/Hub) to convert the signal into a network packet and then send it out.

Tip: When you see a new protocol, use these three questions to ask: "What is it used to transmit?" "Does Home Assistant have corresponding integration?" "Do I need to buy an extra box in the middle?" After answering these three questions, you will probably know whether it is worth buying.

Do it yourself: Take stock of which agreements your home currently runs

Before buying something new, figure out what you already have. These five steps can be completed in about ten minutes.

  1. Open the list of integrations and see what's already there

    Left menu point Settings, enter Devices and Services. This page lists all connected integrations. Copy the names that appear in it. For example, if you see "MQTT", "Zigbee Home Automation" and "Bluetooth", it means that your home is already running these protocols.

  2. Check if there is a dedicated Zigbee or Z-Wave page

    If you have plugged in the Zigbee coordinator and set up ZHA, Settings There will be one more in the menu Zigbee Item; this will also appear after Z-Wave is set up Z-Wave item. The presence of these two entrances is the fastest way to determine "whether my home has this network." The menu position of different versions may be slightly adjusted, whichever you see on the screen.

  3. Count how many Wi-Fi devices there are

    Log into your router's management page and look for a list of "Connected Devices" or something similar. Count the ones that aren't phones, computers, TVs—those are your Wi-Fi smart devices. If this number has exceeded a dozen units and you plan to continue adding more, then it is time to consider switching to Zigbee or Thread.

  4. Mark each device as "local" or "cloud"

    return to Settings > Devices & Services, integrate point by point. The judgment method is very simple: if you are required to enter the account and password of the manufacturer's App during the initial setting, or jump out of the webpage and go to the manufacturer's official website for authorization, then it is most likely the cloud; if you are only asked to fill in a local network IP or it is automatically found directly, then it is the local one.

  5. Do a drill to disconnect from the internet

    Unplug the router's external network cable (not turn off Wi-Fi, but unplug the external one) to maintain your home's internal local network. Then try turning each device on and off with Home Assistant. What moves is true local, what spins around and then fails is cloud dependence. Remember to plug the cord back in after the exercise.

  6. Record the result

    use Chapter 4 The Label function is taught to add a "Cloud" label to the devices that the cloud relies on. When there is a power outage or Internet outage later, you will know at a glance what will fail and who to replace first next time.

Zigbee in depth: Coordinators, routers, channel jamming

Zigbee devices are widely available in many markets, often inexpensive, and commonly battery-efficient. But it has a few landmines. Stepping on them will make you think "Zigbee sucks", but in fact it's just that the layout is not done right.

Three roles: coordinator, router, terminal device

rolewhat isTypical examplePay attention to
Coordinator CoordinatorThere can only be one brain for the entire network in the whole family.USB receiver plugged into HA hostIt hangs down the entire Zigbee network and shuts down
Router RouterA relay station that helps forward signalsZigbee light bulbs, sockets, and switches with always-on powerA device must be "always powered" to function as a router
End Device End DeviceOnly responsible for repaying, not forwarding for othersBattery door and window sensors, thermometer and hygrometer, buttonsIt won't extend your network coverage
Concept: Home Assistant documentation states that Zigbee router devices "must remain available at all times." Therefore, the correct solution to expand Zigbee coverage is not to buy a stronger coordinator, but to install a few more plugged-in Zigbee sockets or light bulbs in the middle corridors and stairwells so that the signal has a place to be relayed.

How to choose a coordinator

Home Assistant official documentation currently list several series of recommended coordinators: Silicon Labs EmberZNet chip models (such as Home Assistant Home Assistant Connect ZBT-2, Connect ZBT-1, Home Assistant Yellow, SONOFF ZBDongle-E, SMLIGHT SLZB-07), Texas Instruments' CC2652 and CC1352 series USB sticks, and dresden elektronik's ConBee III.

The document also lists a number of old models as no longer recommended, including ConBee II, RaspBee II, SONOFF ZBBridge, Nortek HUSBZB-1, Elelabs series, Digi XBee series, ZiGate series, and TI's CC2530/CC2531/CC2538. There are many of these on online auctions and second-hand markets, and the price is often only one-third of the recommended model. If you see a particularly cheap model, check to see if the model is on the "not recommended" list before placing an order.

Home Assistant specification of Connect ZBT-2 is Silicon Labs MG24 chip with ESP32-S3 responsible for USB connection. The transmission rate is 460800 (the previous generation ZBT-1 is 115200). It uses an external antenna instead. Home Assistant recommended price is US$49/€45. The box comes with a 1.5-meter USB-C cable.

Warning: ZBT-2 can only run one protocol at a time. Home Assistant documentation is very clear: it cannot function as a Zigbee coordinator and Thread border router at the same time. Either use it to run Zigbee or use it to run Thread. If you want both, be prepared to buy two.

Channel interference: Don’t rush to change the Zigbee channel

Zigbee and Wi-Fi are both squeezed into 2.4GHz, so interference with each other is the norm. However, the suggestions in Home Assistant ZHA document are contrary to many online teachings. It says: Best practice is not to change the Zigbee channel. ZHA's "Smart" option will scan all channels when establishing a network, giving priority to 15, 20, and 25. This is a one-time action.

If there is really constant interference, the order suggested by the document is: first place the coordinator away from Wi-Fi base stations and other Wi-Fi signal sources. If that still doesn't work, change it. Your router’s Wi-Fi channel, instead of changing the Zigbee channel. The reason is practical: changing the Zigbee channel usually means re-pairing all the devices, an afternoon's work.

USB 3.0 is the invisible killer

Danger: Home Assistant documentation clearly warns: Zigbee USB receivers can only be plugged into USB 2.0 ports, not USB 3.x ports. USB 3.0 will generate a lot of noise in the 2.4GHz band, which will directly destroy your Zigbee network. If your host only has USB 3 ports, buy a powered USB 2.0 hub to convert them.

In addition, it is strongly recommended to use a USB extension cable to pull the receiver away from the host body, because the host, hard disk, and SSD themselves are all sources of interference. The document reminds you to choose extension cords that are well shielded. Generally, the thicker the wire, the better the shielding.

Suggestions for placement

  • The coordinator is placed in the house central location, do not stuff it into the innermost part of the TV cabinet or the metal cabinet.
  • Stay away from electrical appliances and lots of wires. Refrigerators, microwaves, and power supplies are all bad places.
  • For large square footage or long layout, first install a plug-in Zigbee device in the middle as a relay point, and then expand outward.
  • ZHA users can Settings > Zigbee Select "Show map" to see the network topology. The RSSI and LQI values above can help you find which section of the connection is particularly weak.

Tip: The choice suggestion is simple - choose ZHA for the first time, because it has two less things that can break. Wait until one day you encounter "ZHA cannot recognize this sensor", then consider moving to Zigbee2MQTT. Both sides use the same coordinator, and the main cost of replacing it is that all devices need to be re-paired.
Warning: Never open ZHA and Zigbee2MQTT at the same time to grab the same USB receiver. One coordinator can only be used by one software at the same time. If two are opened at the same time, neither side can use it.

The Zigbee2MQTT official document currently lists two types of receivers, zStack (Texas Instruments) and EmberZNet (Silicon Labs), as recommended - there is a high degree of overlap with ZHA's recommended list, so if you buy a good coordinator, both paths will work. In terms of installation, Zigbee2MQTT is installed as an App (formerly called add-on) on Home Assistant. In addition, a MQTT broker is required. Mosquitto is officially recommended.

What should I do if I change the coordinator?

ZHA has built-in migration capabilities in Settings > Zigbee There is a "Migrate" item, and official documentation describe support for migration between models using Silicon Labs, Texas Instruments, and ConBee/RaspBee chips. This means that when you switch from an old receiver to a new one, you have the opportunity to not have to reconfigure your entire home's sensors. Please follow the wizard on the screen of your version for the actual process.

Z-Wave: more expensive, but stable

Z-Wave availability and pricing vary by market. It is a mainstream choice in Europe and the United States because it does not squeeze 2.4GHz with Wi-Fi, and the interference problem is naturally reduced by more than half.

Find out the frequency band before buying

Note: This English edition generalizes the zh-TW edition’s Taiwan-specific example. Check the services, regulations, frequencies, prices, and availability in your region. Z-Wave uses frequency bands below 1GHz, and Different regions use different frequencies. European, American, and Australian devices cannot communicate with each other at all. Before placing an order from a foreign website, be sure to confirm that the coordinator and device you buy are the same regional version. The wrong purchase is not "less effective" but "completely unable to connect". Regional versions vary by market, so confirm the exact frequency with the seller.

How to run in Home Assistant

Behind Home Assistant’s Z-Wave integration is Z-Wave JS. After setting it up, Home Assistant documentation says you can Settings > Z-Wave or Settings > Devices & Services > Z-Wave Enter the management page. It will use the "App" of Z-Wave JS - this thing was formerly called "Add-on". Home Assistant officially renamed Add-on to App in version 2026.2, and the menu path becomes Settings > Apps (The English interface is Settings > Apps; the translation of the Chinese interface may be slightly adjusted, and you will see "App" or "App" in the same place). This part is in Appendix A There are more complete instructions.

Hardware and add-on devices

Home Assistant document recommends that people who are just getting started buy it directly. 800 series receiver and update the firmware to 7.23.2 Above; Home Assistant Home Assistant Connect ZWA-2 is also named in the document. In addition, old models such as Aeotec Z-Stick Gen5+, Zooz ZST10, and Nortek HUSBZB-1 are also on the usable list, but they are "still running" and not "recommended to buy new".

There are two ways to add a device to the network (termed "inclusion"):

  • SmartStart: Scan the QR code on the device, and as soon as the device is powered on, Home Assistant documentation says it will automatically join within 10 minutes.
  • tradition incorporated: Switch both the host and the device to inclusion mode. For how to enter the inclusion mode on the device, you must check the instruction manual of the device. Each manufacturer's method is different.

In terms of security, Z-Wave supports S2 encryption. Official documentation mention that S2 will not cause additional network traffic burden and can bring additional benefits. Security keys are automatically generated when creating a new network. There is also Z-Wave Long Range, a long-distance network type that can be used with supported devices.

Matter and Thread: One is language and the other is road

These two words are most often confused, but they can be clearly distinguished in one sentence: How does Thread transmit (the radio layer), and what does Matter talk about (the data format layer). Therefore, there are "Matter over Thread" devices and "Matter over Wi-Fi" devices in the world, and both are Matter.

What is Thread border router?

Thread devices form a low-power mesh network by themselves, but they talk about IPv6. You need something to help connect the Thread network to your home Wi-Fi/wired network. That thing is called a border router.

Home Assistant Thread integration documentation currently lists border routers as:

Add Matter devices to Home Assistant

Let me first talk about a premise that many people get stuck on: To pair Matter devices, you must use your phone’s Companion App, which cannot be done using a browser.. Home Assistant documentation makes it clear that Home Assistant’s Matter controller is paired through the Companion App. Companion App is installed in Chapter 7 There is talk.

  1. Install Matter integration first

    Go to Settings > Devices & Services, added Matter integration. If you are running Home Assistant OS, you will be asked if you want to install Home Assistant Matter Server. Click Submit Just let it install itself. This Matter Server is a stand-alone program that is responsible for connecting your Matter network (termed Fabric) with Home Assistant.

  2. Make sure IPv6 is turned on

    Home Assistant documentation lists this as a requirement: IPv6 must be enabled. Check the path is Settings > System > Network, set IPv6 to Automatic or static. If this step is not done, subsequent pairing will fail inexplicably.

  3. Open the Companion App on your phone

    In the Home Assistant App on your phone, enter Settings > Matter, point Add new device. Android users must also set the app's location permission to "Always Allow", otherwise the device cannot be scanned.

  4. Select whether this is a new or used unit

    If the device is brand new and has not been added to another platform, select "No, it's new." and then scan the QR code on the device with your phone's camera, or click "More Options" to manually enter the pairing code. Finally press Join Home Assistant.

  5. If the device is already on another platform

    If the device is already added to Apple Home or Google Home, select "Yes, it's already in use," indicate which controller it is currently connected to, and follow the on-screen instructions. The actual process is to generate a set of QR codes or pairing codes for sharing in the app of that platform, and then get them to Home Assistant for use. One of the great things about Matter is that the same device can be controlled by multiple platforms at the same time.

Warning: Matter relies on local network discovery packets such as mDNS/multicast. Official documentation require that this type of traffic must be able to pass freely through your network. If your home has VLAN network switching, or the router has "AP isolation" enabled, Matter will almost certainly fail to pair. In addition, when pairing the Wi-Fi version of Matter device, the phone and the device must be on the same 2.4GHz network.
Concept: The device types Matter supports are constantly expanding. Currently, official documentation lists lights, switches, locks, curtains, fans, air conditioners, water heaters, sensors, binary sensors, buttons, alarms, valves, sweeping robots, electric vehicles, and some general types (values, options, updates). But in practice, "support" does not mean "every function can be used" - many devices have no problem with basic switches, but some fancy functions that are only available in original apps are just not available. Before buying, it is recommended to search the actual returns of that model in the community.

MQTT and local HTTP: two versatile paths for DIY

What I talked about earlier was "some kind of radio". MQTT is different from HTTP, they run on your home network Message format, regardless of whether it’s a Wi-Fi or network cable underneath. These two paths are your lifeline when there is no dedicated integration for a device.

What is MQTT: A community bulletin board

Imagine that there is a bulletin board in a community hall with many grids (the term is called "topic"). Every five minutes on the thermometer and hygrometer, go to the "living room/temperature" box and put a piece of paper on it and write 26.5. Home Assistant only needs to say "I want to subscribe to the living room/temperature grid" in advance, and it will know as soon as someone posts it. This bulletin board is broker (message proxy server).

The advantage of this design is that the person posting the sticker does not need to know who is reading it, and the person views it does not need to know who is posting it. So you can add unlimited devices and unlimited subscribers without knowing each other.

Install a broker

Home Assistant MQTT document says that the easiest way is to install Home Assistant Mosquitto Broker App (also known as Mosquitto add-on), and it can be automatically installed during the process of setting up MQTT integration.

The path to add MQTT integration is Settings > Devices & Services, lower right corner New integration, select from the list MQTT.

Automatic discovery: The most fascinating part

Concept: Official documentation describing MQTT automatic exploration It is on by default, the default prefix for exploring topics is homeassistant. This means that the device only needs to follow the agreed format and go to homeassistant/... Drop a self-introduction message into the opening topic, and Home Assistant will automatically create an entity for it, so you don’t have to set it manually at all.

This is why Zigbee2MQTT and Tasmota are so smooth to use - you pair a new sensor with them, and Home Assistant will appear on its own in a few seconds.

After setting it up, you need to go back and change things. Home Assistant documentation divides it into two things:

  • Change the broker’s connection information (Change IP, change account password): Settings > Devices & Services, select MQTT integration, click the three-dot menu, and select reset (Reconfigure).
  • Change options like automatic exploration: On the same MQTT integration page, click Set MQTT options (Configure MQTT Options) button.

The actual button text will vary depending on the version and language. It should be based on what you see on the screen - look for the "three-dot menu" and "button on the integrated page" to avoid getting lost.

Local HTTP: last resort, but useful

Some devices do not support any protocols, but they have a web interface or provide a URL for you to call. It can be used at this time rest_command This integration creates its own API. It can currently only be written in configuration.yaml Here:

rest_command:
  open_gate:
    url: "http://192.168.1.50/api/open"
    method: post
    verify_ssl: false

The parameters listed in Home Assistant documentation include url(Required, support template),method(Default get, available get/patch/post/put/delete),headers, payload, verify_ssl(Default true), timeout(Default 10 seconds). The example above verify_ssl Turn it off because most small devices in the local network do not have formal credentials; if you are calling an external network service, do not turn it off. After setting it up, call it like this in automation or script:

actions:
  - action: rest_command.open_gate
Tip: Note that what is used here is action: rather than the old way of writing service:. Home Assistant has renamed "Service" to "Action". YAML triggers: / conditions: / actions: They are also in the plural form. Chapter 8 There are full instructions.

Bluetooth BLE: cheap, power-saving, but not far-reaching

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) devices are usually the cheapest, and a button battery can last a long time. The disadvantages are also very direct: it barely penetrates walls, and it does not mesh, so the signal in one room cannot be transmitted to the next room.

Two ways of operation

modeHow it workssuitable for
Passive reception (broadcast)The device itself keeps shouting values into the air, and HA writes them down when he hears them.Devices that only report, such as thermohygrometers and plant sensors,
Active connectionHA actively establishes a connection with the device to ask or issue commands.Bluetooth locks, curtain machines that require commands, and thermostats

Home Assistant's Bluetooth integration offers three scanning modes: Auto (Mainly passive listening, interspersed with short active scanning), Active (continuous and proactive inquiry, the fastest update but more battery consumption of the device), Passive (Listen only, minimal impact on battery). Official documentation say that Auto mode can reduce scanning power consumption by about 95% compared to always turning on active scanning, so unless you have a special reason, just keep it in Auto.

The new path is Settings > Devices & Services > Add integration > Bluetooth.

BLE Proxy: Put ears in every room

Since Bluetooth can’t transmit far, the solution is not to rely solely on the host’s Bluetooth. You can place a cheap little device in each room as a "collector," which listens to the signal using Bluetooth and forwards the data to Home Assistant via Wi-Fi or a network cable. This is Bluetooth Proxy.

Home Assistant Bluetooth documentation states that the integration can receive broadcast data from external repeaters, and names ESPHome's ESP32 devices, Shelly Gen2 and above devices, and SMLIGHT SLZB-U.

Tip: If your home already has several Shelly Gen2 or above smart switches hidden in the wall, they can be used as Bluetooth collectors, which is a free upgrade. This is a hidden feature that many people don’t know about.

Make your own ESPHome Bluetooth Proxy

Buy an ESP32 development board (a low-cost board) and run ESPHome. The core of the configuration file only has these lines:

esp32_ble_tracker:

bluetooth_proxy:
  active: true

Home Assistant ESPHome document explains the key points of this component:

  • Use ESP32 series boards. The document recommends setting the ESP32 platform to esp-idf frame because another arduino The framework consumes much more memory and runs poorly.
  • active What is turned on is the ability of "agent active connection". Different versions of the document have inconsistent explanations of the default value of this option, so as above clearly written active: true The safest thing is that you know what you are prescribing.
  • connection_slots is the number of active connections that can be maintained at the same time, Default 3. The document recommends not exceeding 5, to avoid problems caused by insufficient memory; in esp-idf Under the framework, you can reach up to 9. Each slot consumes approximately 1KB of memory, and this number cannot exceed esp32_ble_tracker It's set there max_connections.
  • Boards that use network cables (such as Olimex ESP32-PoE-ISO) are usually more stable because they don't have to compete with Wi-Fi for the 2.4GHz antenna.
Warning: The connection slot will be used up. A proxy has 3 slots, which means it can maintain up to 3 "actively connected" devices at the same time. If you have five Bluetooth locks in your home, you need to put a few more proxies to share them. It’s just that the thermometer and hygrometer that passively listens to the radio does not occupy a slot. It doesn’t matter if you put dozens of them.

KNX and Modbus: the world of construction and offices

These two are "adults' agreement". You can’t buy them much at online auctions, but if your home is a newly-built house with smart features, or you are responsible for the devices of a company’s office, it is very likely that these two sets are running under the entire building. Their common characteristics are: wired, extremely stable, not broken for decades, but with high thresholds.

KNX: Busbar in the wall

KNX is the European standard for building automation. It uses a dedicated bus line (bus), and all switches, curtains, and air conditioning controllers are hung on this line. Its biggest feature is Can function without a central brain—You set the "button on the wall in the living room" and the "light in the living room" to the same group address. When you press them, the light will turn on, even if the host is hung up.

Group Address It is the core concept of KNX. it looks like 1/2/3 Such a three-segment number. You can think of it like the grid numbers on a bulletin board: press the button to go 1/2/3 If you drop an "on" in this grid, all the lights subscribed to this grid will light up together. Home Assistant KNX integration document explains that the group address is set with a string or integer, and the three-layer structure is 1/2/3 this format.

In Home Assistant, KNX integration can be accessed from the UI settings: to Settings > Devices & Services > Add Integration Choose KNX. Official documentation list three connection methods:

Connection methodwhat to do
AutomaticScan by itself at startup to find the KNX IP interface on the network
TunnelingDesignate to connect to a specific KNX IP interface
RoutingCommunicate with KNX IP router using Multicast

There are also encrypted versions of Secure Tunneling and KNX IP Secure Routing. The document also mentions a KNX panel that can create entities, configure devices, and expose content directly from the front end without having to write YAML by hand.

Concept: KNX item settings are usually done using a set of professional software called ETS, which is a tool for utility/system vendors. If your home was installed by a builder, ask the builder for an ETS project file or at least a group address list. This will save you dozens of hours of guesswork. As for the encryption settings, Home Assistant documentation states that you can export the Keyring file from ETS and then import it into Home Assistant. The path is the three-click radio button integrated in KNX. reset, and then select Import KNX Keyring.

Modbus: the lingua franca of industrial devices

Modbus is the grandfather agreement in the industry. It has been around since 1979. It is still used in electricity meters, frequency converters, PLCs, large air conditioning hosts, and solar inverters. It is extremely simple: there are multiple numbered cells (registers) in the device. You read a numbered cell and get a number, and then you have to know what that number represents.

Modbus has two common connection methods:

  • Modbus RTU: To use the RS-485 physical cable, you usually need to buy a USB to RS-485 converter and plug it into the host computer. The wire can be stretched very long and has good anti-interference.
  • Modbus TCP: Take the network route, the device has its own IP. The setup is much simpler.

Home Assistant Modbus integration documentation is currently Can only write YAML, you need to restart Home Assistant after making the changes to take effect. Supported connection types are tcp, udp, rtuovertcp, serial Four kinds. The minimum settings are like this:

modbus:
  - name: modbus_hub
    type: tcp
    host: 192.168.1.60
    port: 502
    sensors:
      - name: Office master meter
        address: 100
        input_type: holding
        device_address: 1

If it is an RS-485 serial port, change the key fields to this group:

modbus:
  - name: modbus_hub
    type: serial
    port: /dev/ttyUSB0
    baudrate: 9600
    bytesize: 8
    method: rtu
    parity: E
    stopbits: 1

Key parameters and default values listed in several files:

parametersmeaningDefault value
addressThe register or coil address to be read can be expressed in hexadecimal, such as 0x789ARequired
input_typeRead type:discrete_input, coil, holding, inputDepends on platform
slave / device_addressThe number of the device on the bus1
scan_intervalHow often to read (seconds)15

Currently, official documentation say that seven types of physical platforms are supported: sensors, switches, binary sensors, air conditioners, curtains, fans, and lights.

Warning: Home Assistant document has a rule that is easy to step on: At least one entity must be defined under each modbus device, otherwise the entire integration will not be loaded.. So don't write an empty connection setting first and then add it later, otherwise the connection will not be established at all.
Danger: Modbus can be "written". If you write the wrong temporary register to industrial devices, the parameters may be lost, or the devices may be damaged or dangerous. Please only do reading for first time contact (sensors:), you must obtain the device's temporary register lookup table before writing, and confirm it with the device manager.

Five inspections and purchase decision-making process before buying devices

This section is the most practical part of the entire chapter. Save it as a bookmark and run it every time before placing an order.

Five checks

  1. Which agreement is it?

    Look in the product title and specification sheet. I can't find the words "Zigbee", "Z-Wave", "Matter", "Thread" and "Bluetooth". I only write "smart", "WiFi connection" and "phone remote", so I assume it is a Wi-Fi cloud device. When there are different agreement versions of the same model, the letter at the end of the model is usually the key.

  2. Can it operate without relying on the cloud?

    See if "Local control", "Local API" and "Local control" are written. Or look for warning signs in reverse: If the specifications say "Requires use with XX App" and "Requires account registration", it probably depends on the cloud. Matter-certified devices are more secure in this regard because Matter itself is designed to operate locally.

  3. Is there any corresponding integration for Home Assistant?

    First come before ordering home-assistant.io/integrations/ Search the brand name. It is best to have official integration; if there is only HACS community integration, check to see if that item has been updated recently. Be careful if it has not been updated in half a year.

  4. Should I buy another gateway?

    Some brands of Zigbee devices will tie their own gateways. If you already have a universal Zigbee coordinator, in most cases you can pair it directly without buying its gateway; however, some brands will use private instructions to make the universal coordinator only perform basic switching. Before buying, search "Brand Name + ZHA" or "Brand Name + Zigbee2MQTT" to see community feedback.

  5. Can I buy it after it breaks?

    It is better to choose a model that is available in your region and highly discussed in the community than to choose a model with beautiful specifications but only sold by one overseas store. You'll thank yourself three years from now when you add a second one.

Common problems

  1. A device often displays "Offline/Unavailable"

    First, distinguish whether "all devices are dropped" or "only this one is dropped." If everything goes wrong, check the coordinator or the gateway itself (whether the USB is loose, whether it is plugged into the USB 3 port, whether it is occupied by other software). When only one fails, it's usually a signal problem: it's too far from the nearest router device, or it's separated by a load-bearing wall, metal door, mirror, or fish tank. The method is to add a constant power supply device in the middle as a relay point. For battery-powered devices, you must first confirm that the device is not simply out of power—many people spend three hours checking the Internet, only to find out that the battery is out of power.

  2. Zigbee device is only five meters away from the coordinator and still unstable

    Check the USB port. Home Assistant documentation clearly states not to plug in the USB 3.x port. The noise generated by USB 3 will directly interfere with 2.4GHz. Then check to see if you have used an extension cord to pull the receiver away from the console, and if there is a Wi-Fi base station next to the coordinator. ZHA users can go to Settings > Zigbee Open "Show Map" to look at LQI/RSSI. The section with particularly low values is the problem.

  3. Mesh cannot reach the farthest room

    Remember that mesh only relies on "constantly powered" devices for relay, and battery devices will not help forwarding. Therefore, if ten battery-powered sensors are installed in the farthest room, the network will not be able to reach them. The correct solution is to install a plugged-in Zigbee socket or light bulb in the middle (corridor, stairway). After adding, the devices furthest away may need to be re-paired or wait for a period of time before they automatically change paths.

  4. Matter device fails to pair no matter how

    Check item by item according to the necessary conditions of official documentation: Is IPv6 Settings > System > Network Enable (automatic or static); whether mDNS/multicast traffic can pass freely in your network (it will be blocked if VLAN or AP isolation is turned on); whether you are using the phone's Companion App instead of the browser; whether the Android positioning permission is set to "Always allow"; whether the phone is on the same 2.4GHz network when pairing the Wi-Fi version of the device. If you miss one of these five items, you will fail.

  5. I bought the device only to find it was locked by the gateway brand.

    The symptoms are: Although it is a Zigbee device, the universal coordinator cannot be paired, or after pairing, it can only perform the most basic switch, and all advanced functions disappear. This is the result of the manufacturer using proprietary instructions. There are two remedies that can be done: one is to check whether there is a corresponding support file for Zigbee2MQTT (the community often reverses it); the other is to buy its original gateway at a loss to see if there is a local API that can be connected. There is only one long-term solution - search the community for feedback before buying next time.

  6. When the Internet goes down, half of the devices stop working.

    This is a classic symptom of cloud dependence. use The previous network disconnection exercise Find out which ones they are and label them. What can be done in the short term is to change important automation (security, lighting) to be triggered by local devices; in the long term, it can be gradually replaced. Pay special attention to things like "door locks" and "alarms" and never let them rely on the cloud.

  7. Modbus settings are written but not integrated.

    The most common reason is: only the connection settings are written but no entities are defined. Home Assistant documentation says that each modbus device must have at least one entity, otherwise the integration won't load. In addition, Home Assistant must be restarted after changing the Modbus settings, not just reloading. The device path of the serial port (/dev/ttyUSB0) may also change due to the plug-in sequence.

FAQ

Can I use several protocols at the same time? Will there be a fight?
Yes, and almost everyone ends up mixing it. The entire design of Home Assistant is to put devices with different protocols into the same interface. In practice, there is no conflict at all - you can use a Bluetooth temperature and humidity meter as a trigger condition in the same automation to turn on a Zigbee light and push it to the phone at the same time. The only thing to pay attention to is the interference at the radio level: Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Thread, and Bluetooth are all crowded at 2.4GHz. When there are too many and too dense devices, pay attention to the placement of the coordinator.
Matter is out, will Zigbee be eliminated? Do I still want to buy it now?
Don't worry about it in the short term. The quantity, price, and availability of Zigbee devices are currently the most advantageous, and Home Assistant’s support for Zigbee is very mature. Matter and Thread are good directions, but the ecosystem is still growing, and advanced feature support for some models may not be complete. A more prudent approach is: if you need a large number of sensors now, use Zigbee. If something you happen to want to buy has a Matter version and the price is not expensive, then buy the Matter version. It’s absolutely no problem to have both sets coexisting.
Do I have to use Home Assistant Zigbee coordinator? Can I use cheap online photography?
It doesn’t have to be official. Home Assistant's ZHA document has a clear list of recommendations, including Silicon Labs' EmberZNet series (such as Connect ZBT-2, ZBT-1, SONOFF ZBDongle-E, SMLIGHT SLZB-07), Texas Instruments' CC2652/CC1352 series, and ConBee III. All models on the list can be purchased with confidence. What to avoid are older models that are listed as no longer recommended in the documentation, such as ConBee II, RaspBee II, SONOFF ZBBridge, HUSBZB-1, ZiGate series, etc. The ones that are especially cheap on online auctions are often these old models.
I saw the tutorial saying that I need to go to "Settings > Add-ons", but I can't find it on my screen?
Home Assistant officially renamed "Add-on" to "App" in version 2026.2, and the menu path became Settings > Apps (The English interface is Settings > Apps, and localized labels may vary). Home Assistant reason is very straightforward: most people know what an “App” is. This is how it feels when you open the App store on your phone, pick one, and install it. So when you see the place where Add-on was written in the old teaching, you now have to go to the app to find it. The thing itself has not changed. Mosquitto, Z-Wave JS, and OpenThread Border Router are all still there. By the way, the same version also sets the new "Home Dashboard" as the default for new installations. The old version of the Overview template can also be downloaded from Settings > Dashboard > Create Select "Overview (legacy)" to get it back.
The Zigbee channel is fighting with Wi-Fi. Should I change the Zigbee channel?
Home Assistant ZHA documentation suggests just the opposite: the best practice is Don't Go and change the Zigbee channel. ZHA's "Smart" option will scan all channels and prioritize 15, 20, and 25 when establishing a network. If there is continuous interference, the recommended order is to first move the coordinator away from the Wi-Fi base station. If that still doesn't work, change the Wi-Fi channel of your router. The reason is practical: changing the Zigbee channel usually requires all devices to be re-paired, which is too expensive.
How many Bluetooth devices can an ESP32 Bluetooth Proxy connect to?
There are two situations. There is almost no limit to the number of devices (such as thermometers and hygrometers) that can passively listen to the broadcast, because there is no need to establish a connection at all. Only devices that require "active connection" will occupy the connection slot, the ESPHome documentation says connection_slots The default is 3. It is recommended not to exceed 5 to avoid memory problems. Use esp-idf There can be up to 9 frames, and each slot consumes approximately 1KB of memory. So if you have a lot of devices that need to be actively connected, the correct solution is not to maximize the number of slots on a proxy, but to put a few more proxies scattered in different rooms.
Is it possible for my average family to use KNX and Modbus?
KNX usually appears in newly built items or commercial offices that are equipped with smart devices. Generally, the probability of self-installation of apartments is low. But if your home is pre-sold and has a central control panel when it is handed over, it is worth asking the builder if it is KNX. If so, you can connect it entirely to Home Assistant, which will be a hundred times easier to use than the app provided by the manufacturer. The chance of using Modbus is relatively high - many solar inverters, energy storage devices, distribution box meters, and central air conditioners in buildings are all Modbus. If you want to see electricity consumption data in Home Assistant, you are likely to encounter it.
MQTT sounds very versatile, so should all my devices use MQTT?
no. MQTT is a "messaging method", not a radio protocol. It itself cannot give your light bulb extra communication capabilities out of thin air. Its purpose is: when a system (such as Zigbee2MQTT, Tasmota, or a Raspberry Pi program you wrote yourself) needs to exchange data with Home Assistant, MQTT provides a clean channel and has an automatic discovery function so that the device can emerge on its own. If your device already has official integration, just use the integration directly. There is no need to use MQTT for the sake of using MQTT. It is just an extra middle layer that will break.
I want to use Zigbee and Thread at the same time, is a USB stick enough?
In the case of Home Assistant Connect ZBT-2, the answer is not enough. Home Assistant documentation clearly states that it cannot run Zigbee and Thread at the same time, and can only choose one at a time. So if you want both networks, the simplest way is to prepare two receivers. Another direction to save money is: if your home already has devices such as HomePod mini, Apple TV 4K, and Nest Hub 2nd generation, which themselves are Thread border routers, then the USB stick can concentrate on running Zigbee.
