Chapter 7

Notifications

Install the Home Assistant Companion App, connect your phone, and send a test notification. This completes the path from “Home Assistant detected something” to “you actually know about it.”

Why notifications matter

  • The washing machine finishes → your phone chimes.
  • A door is left open → you get a reminder after leaving home.
  • Power returns after an outage → you know even while you are at work.
  • A camera detects a person → the alert includes a thumbnail.

The Companion App delivers these alerts and is also the most convenient way to use Home Assistant every day.

This chapter triggers a notification manually. The next chapter puts notifications into automations—for example, “Send a notification when the washing machine stops” and “Turn on the lights and notify me when someone comes home.”

Install the Companion App

  1. Find Home Assistant in your app store

    iOS: Search the App Store for Home Assistant.
    Android: Search Google Play for Home Assistant.

  2. Open the app and connect to Home Assistant

    The app scans your local network for a Home Assistant server. Select it if it appears. Otherwise, enter http://homeassistant.local:8123 or your own Home Assistant URL.

What else does the app provide? In addition to push notifications, the Companion App can send battery, location, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth sensor data to Home Assistant. These become entities such as sensor.yourname_battery_level and device_tracker.yourname_phone. You can use them later for arrival automations and low-battery reminders.

Connect your phone

  1. Enter username and password

    use Chapter 5 Create a personal account (do not use a shared administrator account) - so notifications can be sent to you by name.

  2. Permissions to allow app access

    The app requests access to notifications, location, Bluetooth, camera, and other features. Enable only what you need, including at least:

    • Notifications — This is the focus of this chapter and must be opened.
    • location (always) — "Go home" and "leave home" the soul of automation.
    iOS reminder: iOS may initially offer only “While Using the App.” After setup, open iOS Location Services and select “Always” if you want background location updates.

  3. Confirm that your phone appears in Home Assistant

    Open Settings → Devices & services. Your phone appears as a device with entities for battery, charging state, Wi-Fi connection, Focus mode, and other enabled sensors.

Send your first notification

Terminology update: Developer Tools moved under Settings; in 2026.8 its name became Tools . The current path is Settings → Tools → Actions, while older releases use Developer Tools → Actions, with equivalent tabs. Home Assistant renamed “Service” to Action in 2024.8; current YAML uses service: instead of action:.

  1. Settings → Tools → Actions

    Open Settings in the sidebar, select Tools, then open Actions.

  2. Select the action notify.mobile_app_XXX

    XXX is the phone you just connected. The Companion App creates this notification action automatically.

    Notification action naming: notify.mobile_app_<device name>, where non-alphanumeric characters in the device name become underscores. For example, iPhone 13 Pro Max becomes notify.mobile_app_iphone_13_pro_max. In Settings → Tools → Actions, search for notify.mobile_app to find your phone.

  3. Fill in message and title

    Use this test:

    action: notify.mobile_app_xxx
data:
  title: "Test notification"
  message: "First message from HA"

  4. Click "Execute Action"

    Your phone should chime immediately. Home Assistant can now get your attention instead of merely recording an event.

Verify: The test notification should appear within a few seconds. If it does not, check app permissions, Focus or Do Not Disturb settings, and the phone’s internet connection.

Advanced: Notifications with action buttons

A notification can offer action buttons instead of displaying only static text:

action: notify.mobile_app_xxx
data:
  title: "Someone rang the doorbell"
  message: "Tap an action below to choose whether to open the door."
  data:
    actions:
      - action: "OPEN_DOOR"
        title: "Open door"
      - action: "IGNORE"
        title: "Ignore"

An automation can listen for the mobile_app_notification_action event. When it receives action: "OPEN_DOOR" it can run the corresponding action. Treat anything that unlocks or opens an entry as security-sensitive and require appropriate safeguards.

Two common pitfalls:
  1. Keep the number of action buttons small. Layout and display limits differ across Android and iOS; three or fewer works well across both.
  2. Use a distinct action ID. If two doorbells reuse action: OPEN_DOOR, either can trigger the same listener. Make the value unique with {{ context.id }} or a location prefix such as action: OPEN_DOOR_FRONT.

Tags and groups: manage multiple notifications

  • tag: laundry: A later notification with the same tag will replace the previous one, which works well for washer progress updates.
  • group: security: Related notifications are stacked together such as doorbell and alarm alerts.
Advanced application notice: A camera notification can include an image and safe actions such as opening the camera view. Test harmless actions before connecting a lock or alarm.

FAQ

Why did the notification not arrive?
Check the phone’s notification permission, the Companion App notification settings, the selected notify action, and Focus, Do Not Disturb, or battery-saving settings.
Can I notify every phone in the household?
Yes. Use notify.notify only if its configured targets are correct, or create a named notification group for specific recipients. A named group makes it clear who receives the message.
Can I send notifications through email, Slack, or Telegram?
Yes. Home Assistant supports other channels, including SMTP and Telegram. LINE Notify has been discontinued, so international examples should use a supported channel such as Telegram or Slack. Learn the Companion App first, then add another channel if you need a backup.

Troubleshooting

The phone receives no notifications
Check the phone’s notification permission, Focus and power-saving settings, internet access, and the action name. Search for notify.mobile_app in Developer tools → Actions and confirm that your phone is listed.
An iOS notification arrives without sound
Check the iOS notification sound settings for Home Assistant and any active Focus mode. Custom sounds must also be installed and referenced correctly under push: and sound:.
Notifications arrive at home but not while I am away
Companion App push delivery uses Apple or Google push infrastructure, so the phone needs internet access but does not need a remote Home Assistant URL merely to receive a push. Opening the app and controlling Home Assistant while away does require remote access; see the appendix.
