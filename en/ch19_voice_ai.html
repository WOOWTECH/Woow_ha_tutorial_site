Chapter 19

Voice assistant and LLM serial connection

This chapter upgrades your home from "clicking with your mobile phone" to "shouting with your mouth". You will learn how Home Assistant's built-in Assist works, how to decide which devices can understand what you are saying, how to bring speech recognition and reading to your home, and how to connect large language models (LLM) such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude so that it can really turn off the lights for you.

Why should you learn this?

you are alreadyChapter 6Make a good dashboard andChapter 8I have written about automation. But there’s one situation in life that a dashboard can’t save: you walk into the kitchen with a pot of soup in your hands, or you’re lying flat on your bed and your phone has fallen under the bed. What you need at this time is to shout and someone will take care of you.

Home Assistant’s built-in voice assistant calls Assist, it is completely free, built-in, and can be used without installing anything. And its biggest difference from Google Assistant and Alexa is:You can decide whether each segment of it should run in your home or on someone else's cloud.

After reading this chapter you will have these abilities:

  • Know what Assist is and whether it is two different things or the same thing as "LLM".
  • It will set which entities can be controlled by voice and which ones should never be opened.
  • I will set up a voice pipeline (Assist pipeline) by myself, and choose the local or cloud for each of the four sections.
  • Will set the OpenAI/Google/Anthropic model as a conversational agent, and it can really turn on the lights in the house.
  • Know the role of Model Context Protocol (MCP) in Home Assistant.
  • Know where money will be spent, where delays will get stuck, and where privacy will leak.
Concept:This chapter relies heavily on your previous homework.Chapter 3Area (Area) andChapter 4If the naming is not done well, the voice assistant will be so bad that you will want to smash your phone. A random name = a bad name, this is an iron rule.

What is Assist: First distinguish three nouns

Many people are confused by three words at the beginning. Let’s cut it clean first.

nounvernacular explanationwhere would you see it
AssistHome Assistant The brand name for the entire suite of voice features. Including typing chat and speaking.The conversation icon in the upper right corner and the three-dot menu of the mobile app
Conversation agentThe brain that really “understands what you’re talking about.” It can be built-in, or it can be replaced by LLM.Settings → Voice Assistant → Assistant’s setting screen
Voice pipeline (Assist pipeline)A whole processing line: wake word → speech to text → conversation agent → text to speech.Each "Assistant" in Settings → Voice Assistant is an assembly line

The most important thing:The conversational agent built into Home Assistant is not AI, it’s a sentence comparer.It compares what you say with a large number of pre-written sentence templates, and triggers the corresponding intent (Intent) when compared, such as "turn on the lights in the living room". HassTurnOn.

This has two consequences:

  • Benefits: Extremely fast, completely local, zero cost, no random talking. You tell it to turn off the lights and it turns off the lights.
  • Disadvantages: If you miss the sentence pattern, you just can’t understand it. You say, "Can you please help me make the living room darker," and it might crash. And it’s totally nonchatty.

The built-in agent has been strengthened in recent years. After 2025.9, a "fuzzy matcher" was added. Even if you add a few more redundant words and change the way of speaking, it can still achieve the correct intention. But the official statement is very honest:This function is first implemented in English, other languages will follow. Therefore, if you speak Chinese, you still have to rely on easy-to-pronounce names and aliases to increase your hit rate in the short term.

Tip:Don’t rush to pick up LLM yet. Adjust the built-in agent to "can be called". The built-in proxy is a free, instant, reliable baseline, and the LLM is a bonus. Many people skip the baseline and go directly to LLM, and as a result, their home becomes an expensive and slow light-off machine.

If you want to play it for a while without setting anything up: After logging in to Home Assistant, click the conversation icon (a speech bubble) in the upper right corner and type "turn on the living room light" or speak a sentence in the language you set. This is the smallest usable form of Assist.

There are four sections of the assembly line, and parts can be replaced in each section.

Think of a voice assistant as a factory conveyor belt. You shout a sentence, and finally spit out actions and replies. There are four stations in the middle, and each station can independently choose whether to use local or cloud.

Concept:The four sections can be mixed and matched. A very common and practical combination is "wake words on the device (power saving) + voice-to-text in the cloud (Chinese accurate) + built-in conversation agent (fast and free) + reading aloud in the cloud (good sound)". You don’t have to be all local or all cloud, choosing one or the other is a false question.

If you only type and do not speak (for example, typing and asking Assist in a mobile app), stations 1, 2, and 4 will not start at all, and only the conversation agent will run. So "I want to chat via ChatGPT" and "I want to shout into the speaker" are two very different things.

Role division of local solutions

  • Whisper: Speech to text, universal, with wide language support (including Chinese). The model default for the official Add-on is auto, on an ARM machine like Raspberry Pi, you will choose tiny-int8, other machines pick base-int8. You can adjust it yourself to small, medium, but eating up the CPU will obviously make it heavier.
  • Speech-to-Phrase: Speech to text, but it only recognizes the sentence "things you really have at home", so it is super fast even on weak hardware. The trade-off is that it does not support free speech, andThere is no Chinese in the current language list, which is basically not used by Chinese-speaking families.
  • Piper: Text-to-speech, local reading, fast speed and low resource requirements.
  • openWakeWord: Wake word detection, running on the Home Assistant host. The official statement is that a Raspberry Pi 4 can handle approximately 5 voice satellites (satellites) streaming audio at the same time. If there are more, the detection must be moved to another machine.
Warning:Don’t leave Whisper’s language settings in auto. The official document clearly states that automatic language detection will make every requestmuch slower. Simply specify the language your home speaks.

These four are official Add-ons. After installation, use Wyoming This protocol is discovered by Home Assistant. How to use the Add-on store is already inAppendix A I’ve talked about it before, so I won’t repeat it here.

What understands you: Public entity

This is the most important setting in the entire chapter and the one that most people miss.Home Assistant doesn’t hand over all of your physical presence to the voice assistant.You must explicitly "Expose" them for the voice assistant to see them. This is intentionally safe by design.

  1. Open public settings page

    left column Settings → Voice assistants, then cut to Expose Pagination.

  2. See what's public so far

    The list lists the entities that have been exposed, and the assistants to which it was exposed. The assistant here is not only Assist, if you have connected Google Assistant or Alexa, they will also appear on the same table.

  3. Add the entities you want

    press Expose entities Buttons can be checked to add multiple ones at one time. You can also click on a single entity to fine-tune which assistants it should be exposed to.

  4. Add an alias easily (Alias)

    You can add an alias after clicking on a single entity. The entity is called "living room main light" but if you usually talk about "headlight", then add "headlight" as an alias. You can also click the gear in the upper right corner of the entity's information window → voice assistant Go in and change it.

  5. Remove things that shouldn't be shouted

    Door locks, alarms, electric water heaters, anything that generates heat or has safety concerns, unless you really think about it, take it off the list.

Danger:door lock (lock) and the alarm host (alarm_control_panel) is exposed to the voice assistant, which means that anyone standing outside your window and shouting into the speaker can open your door. Unless you know exactly what you're doing, the default is to keep it private.

What happens if you reveal too much?

The official best practices are very straightforward:Expose only the minimum necessary entities. There are two reasons, and both of them hurt:

When using the built-in conversational agentWhen using LLM as a conversational agent
The more names and aliases there are, the more things need to be scanned for sentence comparison, and the slower the response. If the names are similar, there will be errors. If you call "room light", it will turn on "room air purifier indicator light."Every public entity will be stuffed into the hint sent to the model, directly turned into a token cost, and the model will start to select the wrong device as the list grows. The official recommendation in Ollama's documentation is to experiment with local modelsExposes less than 25 entities.

Aliases for areas and floors

It’s not enough to just make your entities public. You need to be able to say "turn off all the lights in the living room", provided that those lights are really assigned to the "living room" area. You can also add aliases to areas and floors:Settings → Areas, labels & zones, click the pencil on the area card → Add alias; To select the floor, click the three-dot menu next to it → Edit floor → Add alias.

Tip:The officially recommended naming format is "Area + Description + Device Type", such as "Living Room Table Lamp". If there is only one light in that room, just call it "living room light". like Tuya Light Controller 0E54B1 Light 1 With this kind of original name, the voice assistant will definitely not be able to call you. Let LLM really make your lights move

After receiving LLM, many people found that it is very good at chatting, but when you ask it to turn off the lights, it starts to say "Okay, I have turned off the living room lights for you" - and then the lights are still on. The reason is almost always one of the following three things.

First: the control switch is not turned on

The three integrations of OpenAI, Google Generative AI, and Anthropic have one in the settings. Control Home Assistant (Control Home Assistant) options. After opening, the model will get the Assist API of Home Assistant and use it as a tool. If not opened, it is a pure chatbot.

If the version is older, the same position may not be a switch, but a drop-down menu with the options saying "No control" and "Assist" - select Assist It means turning on the control, which means the same thing.

Second: The entity is not disclosed

This is the most common. Official documents all say the same thing about the integration of these three companies: modelOnly "public" entities can be controlled or queried. Turning on the control permission but not exposing any entity is equivalent to giving it a remote control without buttons. Go back to the previous "Public Entity" section and finish.

Third: The model is too small

If you are using Ollama to run local small models, the official documents are very honest: small models are more error-prone than large models, andAfter turning on the control function, small models may not be able to maintain dialogue stably.. The official even suggested a workaround: open two Ollama settings, one with no control and dedicated to chatting, and the other with control and dedicated operation of the device.

Concept:"Control Home Assistant" and "Public Entity" are two independent gates that must be opened at the same time. This double-checking is intentional: You don't want a cloud-based model to turn on all your appliances on a whim one day.

A combination that saves money and is safe: Prioritize local processing

There is one in the assistant's three-dot menu Prefer handling commands locally switch. After opening it, Home Assistant will first let the built-in sentence pattern comparer try it. If the comparison is found, it will be executed directly. If the comparison fails, it will be thrown to LLM.

This trick solves three problems at the same time:

  • Fast: A simple command like "turn on the living room lights" is sent locally with almost zero delay.
  • province: 80% of the daily commands do not call the API at all and do not burn tokens.
  • stable: Turning off the lights is left to a comparator that doesn’t think too much, which is more reliable than leaving it to a model.
Tip:This switch should be on in almost all cases. Unless you just want LLM to completely take over all semantic understanding (for example, you are working in a multilingual family, or your speech rarely conforms to the built-in sentence patterns).

Let LLM use your script

you are hereChapter 10Written scripts can also be exposed to voice assistants. This is actually the cleanest way to "teach LLM new skills": wrap a series of complex actions into a script, give it a human-speaking name, make it public, and the model will call it at the appropriate time. This is much more stable than letting the model piece together ten movements on its own.

Home Assistant officially described it in the blog as "a very thin layer that allows LLM to connect to anything." Official hardware: Voice Preview Edition

If you are too lazy to bother, this is the easiest one. Inside is ESP32-S3 (16MB flash, 8MB PSRAM) plus XMOS XU316 audio chip, which is responsible for echo cancellation, noise reduction and automatic gain - in vernacular, "the TV can still hear you when it is playing sound." It has a dual microphone array and a built-in speaker with a 3.5mm output hole. The body has a physical mute switch, volume knob, LED light ring, and a Grove expansion port for connecting sensors. Dimensions: 84×84×21mm, 96 grams.It doesn’t require Home Assistant Cloud to work, can be run fully locally, but the official reminder is that a complete full local experience requires a relatively capable host (approximately Intel N100 level starting).

Tip:Starting with version 2025.10, each Assist voice satellite can be configuredUp to two sets of wake words, corresponding to two different pipelines. This is super easy to use: for example, "Okay Nabu" uses a local quick light-off assembly line, and "Hey Jarvis" uses LLM's cloud assembly line. One machine has two personalities. Whichever name it says, it will take whichever path it takes. Multilingual families can also use one wake word per language.

By the way: Voice satellites can actively talk to you

Voice devices don’t just passively wait for you to shout.assist_satellite This domain (Domain) provides three actions that can be written into automation:

actions:
  - action: assist_satellite.announce
    target:
      entity_id: assist_satellite.living_room
    data:
      message: The washing machine is ready

The other two are assist_satellite.start_conversation(Take the initiative to start a conversation) Follow assist_satellite.ask_question(Ask you a question and get your answer). The third one is particularly fun: you can create an interaction such as "detect someone opening the door → the speaker in the entrance asks "Do you want to turn on the living room lights?" → you say yes → the lights will turn on."

It’s easy to step into a pit when writing this kind of automation:When it is talking to people, you ask it to broadcast again, the two paragraphs will overshadow each other. Starting from 2026.2, there is an additional set of conditions specifically for voice satellites in the automation conditions (Condition), which can determine whether the device is nowIdle, listening, processing, or answering. Before broadcasting, add a condition of "Broadcast only when idle", so that the family members will not be interrupted in the middle of speaking.

Tip:Starting from 2025.10, there is also a very considerate behavior: when the command you give is executed in the area where the satellite is located (for example, you say "turn on the lights" to the speaker in the living room), it will useOne short beepInstead of saying the entire sentence "I have turned on the living room light for you". Those two seconds saved make a huge difference in physical sensation.

What you should know about token spending

Cloud LLM is billed based on usage, and each company's unit price is constantly changing. I won't write down hard numbers here (it will expire soon after writing them down, so go to each company's official website to see the latest prices). But there are a few principles that will keep your bills skyrocketing:

  • Each sentence will send you a list of public entities.The cost difference between exposing 200 entities and 20 entities is an order of magnitude.
  • Prompt template is too longAlso paying every time. Just write three or five sentences about personality settings, don't write a novel.
  • "Prioritize local processing of instructions" is turned on, the API will not be called at all when turning off and on the lights in daily life. This is the most effective money-saving switch.
  • FirstSet usage limitThen paste the API key. The backends of the three suppliers can be set up.
Danger:The API key is equal to your credit card. Do not post the key in forums, screenshots, GitHub, or messages asking for help with setting up. If the key is accidentally leaked, go to the supplier's backend immediately to cancel and reissue it. Don't take any chances.
Concept:Latency is a physical killer. If it takes more than three seconds from the time you finish shouting until the light comes on, your family will no longer use it, and your work has been in vain. If you can only optimize one thing, optimize the speed of "the five most commonly used sentences", it doesn't matter if the others are slower.

Common stuck levels

  1. Can't call any device, it says "Sorry, I don't know about that"

    Ninety percent of it is the entity that has not been made public. go Settings → voice assistant → public Paginate to confirm that the entity is in the list. The second possibility is that the name you mentioned does not match the name in Home Assistant - it uses the name or alias of the entity to compare, and if it is different by one word, it may not count, so add whatever alias comes to mind. The third possibility is that it is not in the area you thought it was at all.Chapter 3Check.

  2. I don’t know which section the problem lies in

    Use official debugging tools.Settings → voice assistant → Click your assistant → Open the Debug dialog box, select the execution you just performed from the drop-down menu, and you can see how long each paragraph took and what text was recognized. In the icon in the upper right corner of the debugging screen, you can also select Run text pipeline, just type one sentence and run the entire assembly line.

  3. I just want to know whether the sentence has been parsed, not actually executed.

    Use Assist’s sentence pattern testing tool:Settings → Tools → Assist Pagination. It will tell you which intent the sentence was parsed into and which entities were locked, butDoesn't really move. After changing the name and adding an alias, it is the fastest to use it for acceptance.

    If you can't find "Tools", check your version:2026.2 agoThis set of tools is called "Developer tools" and is hung at the bottom of the left column; it will be moved intoSettingsAt the bottom of the page, it's called "Tools". The fastest way to move on the computer is to press Ctrl + K(Mac is Cmd + K) Open quick search and type "tools" or "developer" directly.

  4. it reacts but has no sound

    Check first Settings → system → Internet Is the "Local Network Home Assistant URL" in "Local Network Home Assistant URL" correct? The audio file that is read aloud depends on this URL for the device to capture. If the URL is wrong, there will be pictures but no sound. YAML is useless if you write it purely by hand default_config:, also confirm media_source: There are plus.

  5. I can’t pronounce my name correctly, or I have to pronounce my full name in a garrulous way every time.

    Add an alias.Settings → voice assistant → public → Click the entity → Add an alias. An entity can have multiple aliases. If everyone in your family has different opinions, just add one for each. Chinese is particularly prone to encounter situations where the "colloquial abbreviation" and the "formal name" are different, and aliases are the solution.

  6. False trigger: Call A but drive to B

    This is the complication of too much disclosure + too much similarity in names. The order of processing is: first cut off the entities that do not need to be disclosed, and then rename the remaining ones that are easy to confuse into the "region + description" format. Official best practices also remind that the domain of the entity (Domain) and the device class (Device class) must be set correctly - you cannot ask Assist to "open" a valve classified as a switch.

  7. Local Whisper is so slow that I want to cry

    Three directions: turn language from auto Change to a fixed language (officially, auto will be slower)a lot); adjust the model downward (tiny-int8, base-int8 This int8 compressed version); or convert speech to text into Home Assistant Cloud, leaving the local machine to the conversation agent. Whisper's beam_size The default is 0, which means that it automatically uses 1 on ARM machines and 5 on other machines - the larger the number, the more accurate it is but the slower it is.

  8. There is often no response to the wake-up word, or I wake up by myself in the middle of the night

    First confirm whether your wake word is running on the device (microWakeWord) or on the host (openWakeWord). When running on the host, the official mentioned that a Raspberry Pi 4 can support approximately 5 satellite streams at the same time, and it will start to freeze if it exceeds that. The problem on the device side is probably the microphone gain or noise reduction settings. Use ESPHome to adjust the settings of that device.

  9. LLM says it does but it doesn’t

    Classic scene of model hallucination. Checking order: Integration settings Control Home Assistant Is it turned on → Is the entity public → Is the model too small (local small models may not even be able to hold conversations after the control is turned on). The improvement method is to turn on "Priority to process instructions locally" so that instructions with high certainty do not go through the model at all.

  10. Web pages with embedded sidebars are blank

    The other website uses a security header to prohibit iframe embedding. This is decided by them and you cannot change it. Change a website, or use the "open in new tab" method instead. Also check whether HTTPS/HTTP are mixed.

FAQ

Do I have to pay for Home Assistant Cloud to use voice?
No need. Assist itself is completely built-in for free, and the local Whisper, Piper, and openWakeWord are also free official Add-ons. What you buy by subscribing to Cloud is "you don't have to have a capable host yourself" and "Chinese speech recognition and reading quality". If your Home Assistant runs on a Raspberry Pi and you want to use Chinese, subscribing is usually more cost-effective than upgrading the hardware; if you have an idle small host, going full local is completely feasible.
What is the difference between built-in Assist and ChatGPT? Do I have to take LLM?
The built-in Assist is a sentence pattern comparison. Once the sentence pattern is correct, it will be executed 100%. It is fast, free, and available offline, but it cannot adapt or chat at all. LLM can understand vague teachings, speak human language, and have continuous conversations, but it has to pay tokens every time, is slow, and occasionally hallucinates (it says it does it but doesn't do it). The most practical answer for most families isWant both: Connect LLM as the dialogue agent, and turn on "Prioritize processing commands locally" at the same time. Simple commands are sent locally, and those that do not understand are thrown to LLM.
If I paste the API key into Home Assistant, will my family’s data be used to train the model?
It depends on which provider you use and how the provider's API terms are written. Home Assistant cannot control this matter, and each provider's policies will also change. Please go directly to the provider's data usage policy. What you can control isHow much to give away: Expose only the necessary entities and the model will only see those. Do not disclose door locks, cameras, or anything with a sense of personal status. If you don't accept data leaving home at all, the answer is to run local models with Ollama, or simply use the built-in proxy.
Can I ask the voice assistant to unlock the door or release security?
Technically possible (put lock or alarm_control_panel It’s OK if the entity is made public), but it is strongly not recommended. There is no way to reliably tell who is speaking with voice, and you may be heard standing outside the door and shouting into the window. If you really want to do it, at least use a script to wrap it up, add additional conditions (for example, a certain mobile phone must be at home, within a certain time range), and know clearly what risks you are taking.Chapter 10Scripted writing.
I can’t speak Chinese accurately, what’s the order of optimization?
Follow this order:1.Make sure the speech-to-text engine language setting is Chinese and not auto.2.Change the name of the device to a colloquial language that is easy to pronounce ("living room lamp" is a hundred times better than "living room ceiling lamp set 1"), and add a lot of aliases.Three.If the local Whisper cannot hear accurately with a small model, adjust the model upward or use Home Assistant Cloud's speech-to-text instead (zh-TW is in its list).Four.If that still doesn’t work, replace the conversational agent with an LLM and let the model tolerate the identified typos. By the way, the local Speech-to-Phrase currently does not have Chinese in the language list, so there is no need to try this method.
Assist doesn't understand a specific teaching method of mine, can I teach it myself?
Yes, and there are two ways.If you don’t want to touch YAML, go this way:Arrive Settings → Automation and scenarios → Build automation, select from the drop-down menu of Trigger Sentence, fill in the sentence you want to say, and pick up what you want to do on the action side. This is equivalent to using automation to teach it a new line, just click the whole process.If you want to write a configuration file, go this way:in configuration.yaml To extend an existing intent:
conversation:
  intents:
    HassTurnOn:
      - "activate [the] {name}"
A more complete approach is to open it in the settings folder config/custom_sentences/<language code>/ Table of contents (English is config/custom_sentences/en/) into a YAML file, you can define your own intentions, lists, value ranges, and even change it back to your sentences (put one in the same directory responses.yaml). It is safest to restart Home Assistant after making changes.
Can one speaker serve me who speaks Chinese and my family who speak English at the same time?
Yes. Starting with version 2025.10, every Assist voice satellite supportsUp to two sets of wake-up words, each corresponding to an assembly line. You can put "Okay Nabu" on the Chinese assembly line and "Hey Jarvis" on the English assembly line. The same mechanism can also be used to divide "local express version" and "cloud LLM version", depending on which name you call determines which path to take.
Should MCP be touched?
If you just want to use your voice to turn off the lights at home,No need to touch at all, the previous content is enough. MCP is suitable for two types of people: those who want to directly operate their homes in external apps such as Claude and ChatGPT (integrated with Model Context Protocol Server, the endpoint is /api/mcp), or want to add additional tools to the conversational agent at home such as memory and network search (integrated using the Model Context Protocol client, added in version 2025.2, currently only supports Tools). Both involve handing over control and minimizing the list of "public entities" before setting them up.
I put the external AI website into the sidebar, but the result is blank. Am I setting it wrong?
Usually not. Many websites will use the browser's security mechanism to explicitly prohibit themselves from being embedded in other people's web pages. This is the other party's decision and you cannot bypass it. Also make sure the protocols are consistent: if Home Assistant uses HTTPS, the embedded URL must also be HTTPS. By the way, as a reminder, in the old teaching panel_iframe: YAML settings have been replaced by the "Web" dashboard (will be automatically migrated when upgrading), please use now Settings → Dashboard → Add new dashboard → web page.
Can I "ask AI to do things for me" in automation?
Yes, and there are two ways. The first one is conversation.process Action, throw a piece of text to the designated dialogue agent for processing, which is equivalent to talking to Assist in a programmatic way. The second article is AI Task(Building block integration added in version 2025.7), provided ai_task.generate_data follow ai_task.generate_image Two actions, specifically used to generate text, structured data or images in automation, such as "take a look at the camera screen and count the number of cars in the lane". This is much cleaner than putting together a bunch of templates by yourself.
