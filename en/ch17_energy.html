Chapter 17

Energy dashboard and ESG Practices

Which one consumes the most electricity among air conditioners, dehumidifiers and electric hot water bottles? This chapter teaches you how to turn your home or office electricity consumption into understandable charts: from selecting the right sensors, setting up energy dashboards, integrating "wattage" into "degrees", all the way to tracking electricity costs and creating a handy energy-saving report. After reading this, you will know where each kilowatt-hour of electricity is spent, and you will be able to use numbers to prove that "we really save money."

Why you should learn this: From "It feels like a waste of electricity" to "I have numbers"

When the electricity bill comes every month, everyone's reaction is usually "Why is it so much again?" and then nothing happens. Because the electricity bill only tells you a total, it will not tell you which machine contributed to the total.

Energy dashboard solves this problem. It is a special dashboard built into Home Assistant. It will draw your electricity consumption data into "how many kilowatt hours you used today", "how does it look like every day this month" and "which device consumes the most". It can also be converted into money by multiplying the electricity price.

Some very touching situations:

  • at home: You suspect that the old refrigerator consumes a lot of electricity, but there is no evidence. Install a smart socket with power monitoring. After two weeks, you will see how many degrees it consumes steadily every day. It will be clear at a glance whether it is worth replacing it with a new one.
  • room: I forgot to turn off the dehumidifier and it ran all day. With the daily electricity consumption chart, you will immediately find out "Why is there an increase of 3 degrees today?"
  • Office pantry: A water dispenser that stays hot 24 hours a day may seem like a few hundred watts, but it may add up to more than a photocopier in a month. This "low-power but all-weather" monster can only be caught by accumulating data.
  • ESG reporting: When a small office needs to submit an energy-saving or sustainability statement, "We have installed smart sockets and changed the water heater in the pantry to keep it warm during working hours, and the monthly electricity consumption has been reduced by X degrees" is much more capable than "We have a propaganda to turn off the lights."
Concept:This chapter does not require you to be able to write programs, but it does require you to be patient. Energy data is different from automation. Automation responds immediately when pressed. Energy data has to "wait" - it takes at least several days to accumulate to see the trend, and it takes a complete cycle (such as a month) to have comparative value.

Let’s first distinguish between two words: power (W) and electricity consumption (kWh)

This is the most important and most confused concept in the entire chapter. Remember one sentence:Power is like speed, power consumption is like distance.

Nameunitwhat is it answeringMetaphor
PowerW, kW"Now" how much electricity is consumed in this second?60 kilometers per hour
EnergyWh, kWh (degree)How much electricity is used in total "over a period of time"Driving 120 kilometers today

Your utility bills you indegree(1 degree = 1 kWh), not in watts. So:

  • A 1000 W electric heater on for 1 hour = 1 degree.
  • A 100 W water dispenser running for 24 hours = 2.4 degrees.More than electric heaters.

Many cheap smart sockets will only report "the current number of watts" (power), but will not report the "accumulated number of kilowatt hours" (power consumption).What an energy dashboard wants most is electricity usage—All the charts of “how many degrees did you use today” and “how much did you spend this month” are based on degrees. This is why you often encounter "I obviously have power monitoring, but my socket cannot be selected on the dashboard."

Concept:The power sensor is not completely useless. The official document says "If the attributes are correct, you can add the power sensor directly" - it will feed the "Now" page, allowing you to see who is drawing power at the moment. But to do daily/monthly statistics and costs, you still need to have a degree. Therefore, the main official advice given on the individual device page is stillConvert wattage to degrees using Riemann sum integral. The method is later W becomes kWh That section.
Tip:Before buying a smart socket, check whether the specifications or reviews mention "energy / kWh / accumulated electricity consumption." If so, it will save you a lot of setup work.

What does the sensor that can be selected on the energy dashboard look like?

The energy dashboard does not allow you to choose any sensor. It will filter to only list entities with the correct attributes. For a sensor to be selected, three things must be right:

PropertiesWhat to set tovernacular explanation
device_classenergy / power / gas / water / monetaryTell HA "What kind of thing is this number?"
state_classFor accumulation type total_increasing or total;For instantaneous type (power) measurementTell HA "How will this number be counted?"
unit_of_measurementTo be on the legal list, e.g. kWh, Wh, W, kWCapitalization, superscripts and subscripts must be completely correct.

What are the differences between the three state_classes?

state_classmeaningTypical example
measurementThe current measured value is not cumulativeCurrent power is 850 W, room temperature is 26 degrees
totalCumulative value that increases and decreasesThe net electricity consumption of a two-way meter (it will become smaller when the electricity is sold back)
total_increasingThe cumulative value will only increase upwards and occasionally reset to zero.Accumulated power consumption of smart sockets (starts from 0 after power outage)
Concept:official total_increasing The definition is: when the value drops, HA will "interpret it as the beginning of a new counting cycle, or the meter has been replaced." So cutting out the power to the outlet and resetting it to zero won't blow up your chart - HA knows how to deal with that. In addition, it has a built-in tolerance. If the value drops less than 10% between two times, it will not be regarded as zero, thus avoiding misjudgment of decimal point noise.
Warning:Never put device_class: power(Watt) with state_class: total_increasing. Power is not a cumulative value, this combination is not logically valid, and HA will issue a warning in Repairs. Power is measurement.

There is another one in the same areaStatisticsPagination will list all entities with long-term statistics; when the statistics of an entity are broken, a "Fix issue" link will appear there.

In the August 2026 version, "Developer" was removed from the name and it was calledTools—The official statement is that they don’t want non-engineers to be afraid to click when they see “Developer”. Old tutorials on the Internet about "Developer Tools" all talk about the same place. There are seven paginations: YAML, status, action, template, event, statistics, and Assist. Household electricity meter layer

If you want to look at the "entire household" rather than "single appliances", you have to measure at the electrical box level. The method is to clip the CT clip-on current sensor to the main line, and then connect it to a module that can report back to the HA (such as the Shelly series that supports power monitoring, or you can use ESPHome to do it yourself).

Danger:Working on electrical boxes carries risks of electric shock and fire. Although the CT clamp is non-intrusive (clamping it outside does not require disconnection), please leave the opening of the electrical box and fixing the wires to qualified electrical technicians. Renters should also ask their landlord before turning on the main switch. This is not something you can just "try".

Solar energy and energy storage

Most brands of solar inverters have their own API or local interface, and HA has corresponding integrations that can read "current power generation" and "accumulated power generation". Energy storage batteries usually give you two numbers: "accumulated charge" and "accumulated discharge." When setting up the energy dashboard, solar energy and battery are two separate blocks, each feeding different sensors.

Tip:The new version of the energy setting is more tolerant to sensor formats - now you can directly use "a sensor with a positive and negative sign" to represent the two-way flow of mains power or battery, or you can use "two sensors with positive signs" to represent buying/selling electricity or charging/discharging respectively. You no longer need to write a template (Template) to flip the positive and negative signs. In addition, the new version can also help each sourceCustom name, you don’t have to endure canned words like “grid electricity consumption”, change it to “main meter”, “rooftop solar energy”, etc. It’s much easier to recognize when there are multiple sources.
Tip:If you have more than two household batteries, the new version can fill in the "capacity" for each battery, and the combined battery percentage will be weighted according to the capacity. Otherwise, if a small battery is full and a large battery is empty, the average would be a very deceptive 50%.

What to do with only wattage: integrate into degrees using Riemann sum

This is the most common situation: the socket is only for you sensor.xxx_power(unit W), not kWh. It cannot be selected on the energy dashboard.

Solution is built inIntegral / Riemann sum integralAuxiliary components. It will constantly check your power sensor and add up "how many watts × how long it lasts" period by period to become a new cumulative power sensor.

Concept:Why is it called "Riemann sum"? Because what HA gets is not a continuous curve, but discrete sampling points. It can only approximate the real integral by "cutting the curve into many small pieces, calculating the area of ​​each piece, and then summing them up." The more frequent the sampling, the more accurate the estimate.

Create using interface (recommended)

  1. Go to auxiliary components page

    Settings → Devices & services → Helpers tab → Create helper.

  2. Select "Points / Integral"

    The list is a bit long, use the search box integ fastest. The Chinese interface may display words such as "Integral Sensor".

  3. Fill in the input sensor

    For "Input Sensor", select your power sensor whose unit is W. It is recommended that the name be something understandable, such as "Living room air conditioner electricity consumption."

  4. Set metric prefix and time unit

    These two are key.Metric prefix selection k, select Integration time h(hours). W × h ÷ 1000 = kWh, so the unit will be kWh. Prefix selection none If you would get Wh, the number would be 1000 times larger.

  5. Leave the integration method as default

    There are three methods: Trapezoidal, Left, and Right.The default is trapezoidal, officials say it is currently the most accurate when the source updates frequently. Unless you know exactly what you are doing, don't change it.

  6. Set "maximum subinterval"

    "Max sub-interval" is used for devices that "will not report if the value does not change". After setting a value (for example, 60 seconds), the points will advance forward according to time even if the source has not been updated. Set 0 Indicates that this function is turned off. For appliances such as air conditioners and dehumidifiers that maintain a fixed power for a long time, it is recommended to set a value.

Create with YAML

If you prefer to write files, the equivalent writing is:

sensor:
  - platform: integration
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_power
    name: Electricity consumption of living room air conditioner
    unit_prefix: k
    unit_time:h
    method: trapezoidal
    max_sub_interval: "00:01:00"
    Round: 3

The meaning of setting variables:source is the source entity (required),unit_prefix is the metric prefix,unit_time is the unit of time,method It is the integration method,round is the number of decimal places,max_sub_interval It is the forced advancement interval mentioned above.

Warning:The integrated sensor starts from 0.It doesn’t know how much you have used before. Therefore, it can never be compared to an electricity bill, and can only be used to see trends and comparisons. To reconcile, please use hardware that actually has accumulated readings.
Tip:points sensor state_class Fixed is total, no total_increasing. This is as legal as energy dashboards (the official FAQ says "total or total_increasing "Everything is fine"), so don't see that it's not total_increasing Just nervous. In addition, it will automatically help you device_class from source power Replace with energy, you don’t need to set this yourself.

Utility Meter: Divide accumulated numbers into days/months/quarters

After you accumulate points, you will get a number that has been increasing since installation. But what you really want to know is "todayHow many times did it take?"this monthMore than last month, but less." this isUtility MeterWhat the auxiliary component is doing: It's like a stopwatch that automatically resets to zero.

Create using interface

  1. Open the auxiliary component again

    Settings → Devices and Services → Auxiliary Components tab → Create auxiliary components → Utility Meter.

  2. Select input sensor

    Select the kWh sensor that was just accumulated (or the accumulated sensor that is already in the hardware).

  3. Select zero cycle

    "Meter reset period" can be selected every fifteen minutes, every hour, every day, every week, every month, every two months, every quarter, or every year.It is recommended to build three at a time: The day, month, and year are clearly named.

  4. Turn on "Periodic Zeroing" as appropriate

    If the source sensor resets itself (for example, a smart socket restarts after a power outage), make sure "Periodically resetting" is turned on so that the Utility Meter knows how to deal with the dropped number. this optionThe default is onof.

  5. Set offset if necessary

    "Meter reset offset (Offset)" can push the starting point of the cycle back. For example, if your electricity bill is settled on the 5th of every month, set the offset of the monthly cycle to 4 days to align the HA month with the bill.

YAML writing method

utility_meter:
  ac_daily:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living room air conditioner Today’s electricity consumption
    cycle:daily
  ac_monthly:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living room air conditioner Electricity consumption this month
    cycle: monthly
  ac_yearly:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living room air conditioner Electricity consumption this year
    cycle: yearly

Available setting variables include source, name, unique_id, cycle, offset, cron, delta_values, net_consumption, tariffs, periodically_resetting, always_available. Among them delta_values Default false(The source is absolute reading not incremental),net_consumption Default false(only counting forward),periodically_resetting Default true.

Warning:The official statement states that "the first cycle of each sensor is incomplete." If you create "Electricity Usage for the Week" on Wednesday at noon, the numbers for that week are meaningless. The data will only be complete starting from next Monday. Remember to skip the first cycle when making reports.

Electricity Price and Cost Tracking

With the degree, it becomes money when multiplied by the electricity price. Money is much more persuasive than kilowatt-hours - telling your boss, "We spent over 8,000 yuan a year on standby power" is more effective than telling your boss, "We used 200 kilowatt-hours more."

Three ways to count money

After adding a certain power source on the energy settings page, HA will ask you how to track costs. The options are roughly in these three categories:

wayHow it worksSuitable for whom
Fixed unit priceYou just fill in a number of "How much does it cost per kilowatt hour", and HA will use it to multiply the whole process.The vast majority of people. If you want to quickly see approximate numbers, choose this
Use an entity to represent the current unit priceSpecify a sensor, and its value is "How much does it cost per kilowatt hour now?" If the unit price changes, the cost will also change.People who have time-of-use electricity prices or electricity prices that fluctuate over time
Use one entity to represent the total amountYou calculate the accumulated amount yourself and HA uses it directly.Advanced players who already have a ready amount of sensors
Note: This English edition generalizes the zh-TW edition’s Taiwan-specific example. Check the services, regulations, frequencies, prices, and availability in your region. After selecting cost tracking, HA will automatically generate a corresponding "cost" statistic for that energy source, so you don't have to build it yourself. The degree and amount will then be displayed on the dashboard at the same time.

How regional electricity tariffs affect cost tracking

Many residential tariffs have two characteristics that make "a fixed unit price" inaccurate:

  • Progressive step: The more you use, the higher the unit price will be for the excess. Your average unit price will rise based on your monthly usage.
  • Summer month/non-summer month: Rates during summer months are different from other months.
Warning:Rate range, summer month definition and unit price at each levelWill adjust, this chapter deliberately does not write any specific numbers, because the numbers printed in the teaching will definitely expire. Please check the latest electricity price list directly on the official website of your utility provider, or look at the back of your own electricity bill. Use the number you found when filling in the HA.
Tip:The pragmatic approach is:Take the "total amount ÷ total kWh" of your most recent electricity bill, get an average unit price, and fill it in as a fixed unit price.This number is not precise, but it reflects your own actual electricity consumption structure. It is completely sufficient for comparison of "how much money you saved this month compared to last month." Just update it every few months.

Gas and tap water: the other two legs of the ESG audit

If you just want to save money on electricity bills, this is enough. But for a complete ESG or sustainability inventory, electricity is only one component, gas and water also count.

The energy dashboard itself has independent "gas" and "tap water" sections. The setting logic is exactly the same as that of electricity: a cumulative sensor is required.device_class Set to gas or water, state_class use total_increasing or total, the unit is in volume units. After adding it, you can also set the unit price to convert it into money.

The difficulty isHardware. Many residential gas and water meters do not have ready-made smart interfaces. Several common methods listed in official documents are:

practicePrincipledifficulty
Image recognition moduleUse a small lens to take a picture of the surface, and run recognition on the device to read out the numbers., the lens and light source must be fixed
Magnetic sensorThere is a rotating magnet inside the membrane gas meter, and a magnetometer is used to detect the number of rotations.Medium to high, you have to find the right position
Reed switchThe magnet in the detection meter is triggered once every revolution, which is converted into dosage., not every watch has this magnetic point
Radio reception (RTL-SDR)Some electricity, gas, and water meters regularly broadcast readings using wireless protocols, which can be received with a cheap USB TV stick.High, it depends on whether your local watch is broadcasting
Smart meter communication port (P1)Electric meters in some countries have standardized data output ports that can be read directly, and the gas consumption is also spit out.Low, but this is the specification in the Netherlands, Belgium, and Luxembourg. Availability elsewhere depends on the local meter standard.
Danger:gas meterNever dismantle it, don't touch the inside, and don't do anything around it that will produce sparks.. All possible approaches are to "observe from the outside" without touching the pipeline itself. If you have any doubts, don’t do it and instead use manual monthly meter reading to fill in the HA.
Tip:It doesn't matter if automatic meter reading is not possible. Create a "Number" auxiliary component and fill in the number of degrees on the bill every month. After a year, there will be twelve data points, enough to draw trend charts and write reports. There is no shame in having manual data, there is shame in not having data.

ESG Narrative: Turning Data into a Speakable Report

Data alone will not convince;controlOnly then. This section talks about how to turn the graph on the dashboard into a story with a beginning and an end.

Step 1: Establish a baseline (Baseline)

Don't change anything yet, and let the system run for a full cycle - at least two weeks, preferably one month. The number during this period is your "baseline" against which all subsequent improvements will be compared.

Warning:baseline periodDo not take any energy-saving measures. Many people start to check this level and that level as soon as they install it. As a result, there is no clean control group, and it is unclear how much they have saved later. Hold it in.

Step 2: Find the electricity-eating monster

No need for advanced skills, just look at three places:

Where to lookWhat to look forcommon murderer
Individual device rankingsWho has the greatest total degreeAir conditioner, dehumidifier, electric water heater
Electricity consumption in the early morning hoursWhat is it that is still running when everyone is asleep?Water dispenser insulation, set-top box, NAS, old refrigerator
The shape of the daily curveIs there a platform that should be lowered or not?Dehumidifiers and equipment with high standby power consumption that you forgot to turn off
Concept:"Use electricity in the early morning" is the best trick. The electricity consumption at three o'clock in the middle of the night is almost that of your home.Standby base load. Multiply that number by 24 hours by 30 days and you’d be shocked – that’s how much you’re paying for doing nothing. Lowering it usually has the best return on investment.

Step Three: Common Office Improvement Measures

  • Pantry water heater schedule: useChapter 8Learn the automation so that the water heater is only powered on during working hours. The insulation electricity for two to three days on weekends and consecutive holidays is directly saved.
  • Automatic power off after work: Photocopiers, screens, and desktop devices are connected to smart sockets and turned off at 10 p.m.
  • Air conditioner setting temperature control: There is no need to actually change the air conditioner, just measure it first. By looking at "air-conditioning electricity consumption" and "outdoor temperature" together, you can calculate how much you will save by increasing the temperature by one degree.
  • Abnormal power usage notification: Set up an automation that will push a notification when the total power during non-working hours exceeds a certain threshold (see how to do it)Chapter 7). This trick often catches "someone forgets to close".

Step 4: Write a report

A usable energy-saving report only needs these few paragraphs and does not need to be written like a paper:

  1. Measurement method

    Write clearly which circuits or devices you measured, what equipment you used, and the date from which the data was measured. Be honest about the parts you didn't measure (e.g. "Lighting circuit not covered"), which actually adds credibility.

  2. Baseline data

    Monthly power consumption, daily average, and standby base load before improvement. Attached are the monthly review figures for the energy dashboard.

  3. measures taken

    One column at a time, write "what you did" and "when you started" in each column. If there is automation, write down the trigger conditions. This is your execution evidence.

  4. Improved data and comparison

    Electricity usage for the same period length, side by side with the baseline. It is written in two ways: absolute value (how many degrees saved) and percentage (how many percent saved), and the amount conversion is attached.

  5. Honest labeling of distractions

    "This is a non-summer month, the temperature is low, and part of the drop is due to seasonal factors." - A report that writes this sentence is a hundred times more credible than a report that looks perfect.

Tip:To export raw numbers, the energy dashboard chart can be switched to different time ranges and read one by one, or the corresponding Utility Meter sensor can be queried on the History page. If you need to export in large quantities, the community has some custom components for statistics export, but that is beyond the scope of this chapter.

Advanced: Device ranking and solar self-consumption rate

Power consumption ranking of individual devices

Add the kWh sensor of each socket to the "Individual Device" section of the energy settings, and the dashboard will automatically help you sort out who eats the most.

If there are both master meters and sub-devices on the same loop, it will happendouble counting—The total table already contains the usage of sub-devices. The official provides an "upstream device" setting to handle this: you can designate a device as the upstream of another device to form a hierarchy.

Warning:To establish hierarchies,All related entities must be added to the "Individual Device" list first. Only devices that are already on the list will appear in the "Upstream Devices" drop-down menu. If you reverse the order, you won't be able to find the options.

instant power distribution

The new version adds a "Distribution card", which uses a horizontal proportional bar chart to display the numerical distribution between multiple entities. It also comes with an interactive legend - click on the item on the legend, and the percentage will be recalculated in real time. Self-consumption rate of solar energy

If you have solar energy, you will care about one indicator: how much of the generated electricity is used by yourself (self-use), and how much is sold back to the grid.

The energy dashboard itself, once the solar source is set up, will show the amount of electricity produced in relation to what goes in and out of the grid. If you want a clear percentage, you can use the template sensor to calculate it yourself - the concept is "(total power generation − feed power) ÷ total power generation":

template:
  - sensor:
      - name: "solar energy self-consumption rate"
        unique_id: solar_self_consumption_ratio
        unit_of_measurement: "%"
        state_class: measurement
        availability: >
          {{ is_number(states('sensor.solar_production_monthly'))
             and is_number(states('sensor.grid_export_monthly'))
             and states('sensor.solar_production_monthly') | float(0) > 0 }}
        state: >
          {% set gen = states('sensor.solar_production_monthly') | float(0) %}
          {% set exp = states('sensor.grid_export_monthly') | float(0) %}
          {{ ((gen - exp) / gen * 100) | round(1) }}

put sensor.solar_production_monthly and sensor.grid_export_monthly Replace it with your own monthly Utility Meter sensor.availability This section is for foolproofing to prevent the entire sensor from becoming an incorrect value when the source is not ready or the power generation is zero.

Tip:Do not set this percentage device_class. The percentage is not energy or power. Applying a device_class will make HA complain. only give unit_of_measurement: "%" and state_class: measurement Just fine.

How long should the data be retained?

Many people worry about "will my database explode?" Two things need to be distinguished here:

TypeWhat to saveHow long to keep
Recorder historyA complete breakdown of every status changeby purge_keep_days control,Default 10 days
Long-term statisticsAggregated statistical values (this is what the energy dashboard uses)It will not disappear with the general history clearing. You can view monthly reports from long ago.
Concept:So "only ten days left on the history page" is normal, but your energy dashboard can still see the same month last year. The two origins are different. If you want to adjust the number of days that details are retained, please check the settings of the Recorder, but do not adjust it to a large value just to view the energy report, as this will only make the database fatter.

Common stuck levels

  1. My sensor cannot be selected in the drop-down menu of energy settings.

    Ninety percent of the time it's due to unqualified attributes. ArriveSettings → Tools → Status(The old version was called "Developer Tools") Check three things:device_class Isn't it energy(or gas/water),state_class Isn't it total_increasing or total, unit_of_measurement Is it a legal unit (kWh The capitalization of must be completely correct.KWH, kwh None of them count). In addition, the one you chose may be the one W The power sensor is not the power consumption sensor of kWh - the two names are often only one word different.

  2. One day, electricity consumption suddenly surged to several thousand kilowatt hours.

    Typical symptoms are that after the source sensor is reset to zero, it is regarded as "continuous accumulation", or state_class selected total But the device actually resets to zero, and HA counts "jumping from 0 back to a big number" as a huge increment.
    Treat the root cause: put the sensor state_class Change to total_increasing; If using Utility Meter, make sure "Periodic Zeroing" is turned on.
    Treat the symptoms: toSettings → Tools → Statistics, find the entity, use the date and time field to search for the exploded data point, and directly change the value back to a reasonable number. This page is where the official website fixes statistics. When there is an issue with the entity, a "Fix issue" link will appear next to it.

  3. Set it up but the dashboard remains blank

    First make sure you have waited long enough. The statistics are written in hourly units. Of course, they will be blank immediately after setting them. Please wait at least an hour or two. Then make sure there is indeed an "Energy" item in the sidebar - if not, check configuration.yaml of default_config: Has it been deleted? Finally, confirm whether the source sensor is moving now, if it is stuck unavailable or unknown, that's a device connection issue, not a dashboard issue.

  4. Repairs pops up "state class is incompatible with device class"

    The most common is to device_class: power Served with total_increasing. Power is an instantaneous value and can only be used measurement. If the sensor is provided by an integration, go to the integration or device firmware report; if it is a template sensor you wrote yourself, directly change your YAML.

  5. The integrated number is 1000 times larger or 1000 times smaller.

    Metric prefix (unit_prefix) and time unit (unit_time) is not paired properly. Integrate from W to kWh, prefix to k, time required h. Leaving the prefix blank results in Wh. If you find a setting error, delete the auxiliary component and rebuild it. Changing the setting will not go back and correct the accumulated old data.

  6. The appliance is clearly running, but the sensor is not moving.

    The source power sensor may "not report if the value does not change". The points will only advance if there are new sampling points. Go to the settings of the integration auxiliary component and set the "Max sub-interval" to a value (for example, 60 seconds) to force it to advance by time.

  7. Utility Meter's first month numbers are weird

    This is expected behavior, and the official stated that the first cycle must be incomplete. Wait until the next full cycle starts to take a look. When making reports, discard the entire first period.

  8. The cost always shows 0

    Check whether you have actually selected the cost tracking method in the energy settings (the default is not to track). If "Use an entity to represent the current unit price" is selected, confirm whether the unit price sensor currently has a normal value. If it is unknown, multiplied out is naturally 0. In addition, cost statistics, like electricity consumption, will not appear for the first time until one hour.

FAQ

Do I have to buy hardware? Can I use an estimate?
You can make an estimate, but don’t use the estimate for external reporting. The method is to useChapter 12As mentioned, write a sample sensor: when a certain switch is on When outputting the nominal power of the appliance,off When outputting 0, points are taken. This is enough for "how much does this lamp probably eat", but it assumes that electrical appliances always operate at nominal power. The power of air conditioners and refrigerators will cause machine errors to be very large. If you want to convince people with numbers, you still have to really measure them.
The numbers on the energy dashboard don’t match the utility bill. Did I set it wrong?
In most cases it's not a misconfiguration. There are several reasons: first, what you measure is usually only part of the circuit or some electrical appliances, not the total meter of the whole household; second, Riemann sum is an approximation, and there will be errors if the sampling frequency is not high enough; third, the billing cycle is inconsistent - the source example uses a two-month utility cycle with its own meter-reading date, and the HA default is a natural month. The third point can be aligned using Utility Meter's "Offset".

Think of the energy dashboard astrend toolsinstead ofReconciliation tool, the mentality will be much healthier. Its value is "20% less this month than last month", not "exactly the same as the bill".
Which one should be used for "Points" or "Utility Meter"? Do I need to build both?
They solve different problems, and in many cases both are needed.

Integral: Change "watt (W)" into "degree (kWh)". only on your deviceNoAccumulated power consumption, only required for instantaneous power.
Utility Meter: Cut "the accumulated degrees" into "today/this month/this year". The input itself is already kWh.

So the process is: Power → (Integral) → Accumulated degree → (Utility Meter) → Day, month and year segments. If your socket is designed to accumulate degrees, skip the points and connect directly to the Utility Meter.
I have renamed the sensor or replaced it with a new socket. Will the previous historical data be lost?
Statistics are tied to the entity ID (entity_id). Only changedisplay nameDoes not affect history. changeEntity ID If so, HA will usually ask you if you want to move the statistics as well. If you select "Yes", it will be retained.

but if you areReplaced a physical device, that is, a brand new entity and a new count. The old data is still on the old entity, but the old and new data cannot be connected, and the chart will have breakpoints. So you have to think clearly about naming at the beginning - this is exactly whatChapter 4Something I keep stressing. Before changing devices, copy the monthly statistics of the old data and back them up.
If I set up a bunch of sensors, will my SD card or hard drive be overwritten?
There will be an impact, but not as severe as imagined. Power sensors are the type with the largest amount of writes due to frequent changes. A few suggestions: First, the recorder (Recorder) details are preset to only stay for 10 days. This is already a very restrained setting, do not increase it casually; second, the long-term statistics are summarized and are much smaller in size, so don’t worry; third, if you really care, use the Recorder settings to exclude high-frequency sensors that you do not need to see the details, and the statistics will still be recorded as usual.

In addition, if you are still using an SD card to run HA and want to record energy data for a long time, it is recommended that you seriously consider switching to an SSD. SD card life is not friendly to frequent writing. Please read about backupChapter 9.
I have no solar power or batteries and only a few sockets. Is this still useful?
Very useful, and that's where most people start. You can make valuable things with just three sockets: one for air conditioners, one for dehumidifiers, and one for old appliances you suspect. Run for a month and you will clearly see who the protagonist is.

The "Individual Devices" section of the energy dashboard is designed for this scenario, and you don't need a whole-house meter to use it. Once you are familiar with it and are sure that it is of value to you, it is not too late to do a whole-household measurement.
How long does it take to accumulate data before we can begin to draw conclusions?
It depends on what conclusion you want to draw.

"Which appliance consumes the most electricity?": You can get a rough idea in one week, and it’s quite reliable in two weeks.
"My energy-saving measures are effective": Improve at least the month before and the month after the improvement, and pay attention to seasonal interference - compare July with October, more than half of the decrease is due to the weather, not you. The ideal would be to compare a certain month this year with the same month last year, but that would have to wait a year.
"Annual total": To be honest, it takes a whole year. Collect the materials first in the first year, and then compare them in the second year.
