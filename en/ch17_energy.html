Chapter 17

Energy dashboard and ESG practices

Turn power and energy readings into understandable charts. Select suitable sensors, configure the Energy dashboard, convert watts to kilowatt-hours, track local costs, and produce an evidence-based efficiency report.

Why this matters: replace guesses with measurements

A utility bill gives you a total, but it does not show how much each appliance contributed. Without measurements, “Why is the bill so high?” remains a guess.

The built-in Energy dashboard turns consumption data into charts: kilowatt-hours used today, daily usage this month, and usage by device. It can also estimate cost from your configured tariff.

Common uses include:

  • Home: Measure an older refrigerator with an energy-monitoring smart plug. After two weeks, its daily kWh use provides evidence for a replacement decision.
  • Room: A dehumidifier left running all day appears as an unusual increase—such as 3 kWh—on the daily chart.
  • Office pantry: A hot-water dispenser drawing a few hundred watts continuously may use more energy in a month than a copier. Accumulated data reveals persistent loads.
  • ESG reporting: A small office can document a measured result: “After installing monitoring plugs and limiting pantry water heating to business hours, monthly consumption fell by X kWh.” That is stronger evidence than an awareness campaign alone.
Concept: Energy analysis requires time, not programming. An automation responds immediately, but energy trends need several days of data and comparisons are most useful across a complete cycle, such as a month.

This chapter uses helpers from Appendix B and entity naming from Chapter 4. See Chapter 11 for adding devices and Chapter 12 for the sensor domain.

Power (W) and energy (kWh) are different

This distinction is essential: power is like speed; energy is like distance.

Quantity Unit Question answered Analogy
Power W, kW How quickly is energy being used now? 60 kilometers per hour
Energy Wh, kWh How much energy was used over a period? Driving 120 kilometers today

Your utility bills for kilowatt-hours (kWh), not watts. Therefore:

  • A 1000 W electric heater running for 1 hour uses 1 kWh.
  • A 100 W hot-water dispenser running for 24 hours uses 2.4 kWh—more than the heater in the example above.

Some smart plugs report only current watts, not accumulated kilowatt-hours. The Energy dashboard primarily needs energy data for daily use and cost charts. This is why a plug can report power yet remain unavailable in an energy-source selector.

Concept: A correctly classified power sensor can feed the Now view and show current demand. Daily and monthly consumption and cost still require energy in kWh. Convert watts to kWh with an Integral helper as described under W becomes kWh.
Tip: Before buying a smart socket, check whether the specifications or reviews mention "energy / kWh / accumulated electricity consumption." If so, it will save you a lot of setup work.

Requirements for Energy dashboard sensors

The Energy dashboard lists only entities with compatible attributes. Verify these three fields:

Attribute Required value Purpose
device_class energy / power / gas / water / monetary Tell HA "What kind of thing is this number?"
state_class total_increasing or total for cumulative values; measurement for instantaneous power Tells Home Assistant how to aggregate the value.
unit_of_measurement A supported unit, such as kWh, Wh, W, or kW Spelling and capitalization must be exact.

What are the differences between the three state_classes?

state_class meaning Typical example
measurement A current, noncumulative measurement Current power is 850 W; room temperature is 26°C
total Cumulative value that increases and decreases The net electricity consumption of a two-way meter (it will become smaller when the electricity is sold back)
total_increasing A cumulative value that normally increases and may reset to zero Smart-plug energy total that resets after a power failure
Concept: With total_increasing, Home Assistant interprets a decrease as a new meter cycle or meter replacement. A plug that resets to zero after losing power should not create an extreme chart value. A built-in 10% tolerance prevents small decreases from being mistaken for resets.
Warning: Never combine device_class: power (watts) with state_class: total_increasing. Power is instantaneous, not cumulative, and must use measurement. Repairs will flag the invalid combination.

How to check if your sensor attributes are correct?

Open Settings → Tools → States, search for the sensor, and inspect its attributes. The Statistics tab lists entities with long-term statistics and shows Fix issue when a statistics problem is detected.

Warning: This area was named Developer tools and appeared in the sidebar before moving under Settings. In Home Assistant 2026.8 it became Tools. Older tutorials refer to the same seven tabs: YAML, States, Actions, Templates, Events, Statistics, and Assist.

Energy data must come from a meter or sensor. These are common sources, from simpler to more involved.

Household electricity meter layer

Whole-home monitoring requires measurement at the electrical panel. A clamp-on CT sensor surrounds a main conductor and connects to a module that reports to Home Assistant, such as a compatible Shelly device or an ESPHome system.

Danger: Work inside an electrical panel can cause electric shock, arc flash, or fire. A CT clamp is noninvasive, but opening the panel and routing or securing wiring must be left to a qualified electrician under local rules. Renters must also obtain the required property approval.

Solar energy and energy storage

Most brands of solar inverters have their own API or local interface, and HA has corresponding integrations that can read "current power generation" and "accumulated power generation". Energy storage batteries usually give you two numbers: "accumulated charge" and "accumulated discharge." When setting up the energy dashboard, solar energy and battery are two separate blocks, each feeding different sensors.

Tip: The new version of the energy setting is more tolerant to sensor formats - now you can directly use "a sensor with a positive and negative sign" to represent the two-way flow of mains power or battery, or you can use "two sensors with positive signs" to represent buying/selling electricity or charging/discharging respectively. You no longer need to write a template (Template) to flip the positive and negative signs. In addition, the new version can also help each source Custom name, you don’t have to endure canned words like “grid electricity consumption”, change it to “main meter”, “rooftop solar energy”, etc. It’s much easier to recognize when there are multiple sources.
Tip: If you have more than two household batteries, the new version can fill in the "capacity" for each battery, and the combined battery percentage will be weighted according to the capacity. Otherwise, if a small battery is full and a large battery is empty, the average would be a very deceptive 50%.

DIY: Turn on the energy dashboard

Assume you have a plug that reports cumulative energy in kWh. Use these steps to add it to the dashboard.

  1. Confirm that the sensor is qualified

    Open Settings → Tools → States and select the plug’s cumulative kWh sensor, not its W sensor. Confirm device_class: energy, state_class: total_increasing, and unit_of_measurement: kWh.

  2. Open the energy settings page

    go Settings → Dashboards → Energy → Edit dashboard. There are a total of six sources that can be set here: Electricity grid, Solar, Battery storage, Gas, Water, and Individual devices.

    Version note: Starting in March 2026, the settings are divided among Electricity, Gas, and Water tabs instead of one long page. The available sources are unchanged.

  3. Add grid consumption first

    Select "Add consumption" in the "Power Grid" section, and then select your sensor from the drop-down menu. If your sensor does not appear in the drop-down menu, 80% of the time one of the three attributes in the previous step is wrong. Go back and look. Qualified sensor That section.

  4. Set electricity price (can be skipped first)

    The dialog also asks how to calculate cost. You can skip cost tracking and add it later. See Electricity prices and costs for the available methods.

  5. Add a single appliance to "Individual Devices"

    To compare an air conditioner with total household use, configure the grid meter and add the air conditioner’s kWh sensor under Individual devices.

  6. Save and allow statistics to accumulate

    After setup, Energy appears in the sidebar. An initially empty dashboard is normal. Statistics are aggregated hourly, so allow at least one or two hours for the first bar and a full day for a useful daily chart.

  7. Come back the next day to adjust the layout

    After you have the data, select "Customize cards" in the toolbar of the energy dashboard, expand each section (Overview, Electricity, Gas, Water, Now), and use the switch to switch whether to display each card. Turn off unused blocks so that the screen will not be cluttered.

    By the way, the second tab of the energy dashboard will be renamed from "Energy" to " Electricity "—Because the entire dashboard originally manages electricity, gas, and water at the same time, the old name is easy to misunderstand.

Warning: If Energy is absent from the sidebar, confirm that configuration.yaml still contains default_config:. Removing it disables multiple built-in integrations.

Convert watts to kWh with a Riemann-sum integral

A common case is a plug that provides only sensor.xxx_power in W, not cumulative kWh. That sensor cannot serve as a cumulative energy source.

Use the built-in Integral helper. It integrates power over time—watts multiplied by elapsed time—to create a cumulative energy sensor.

Concept: Home Assistant receives discrete samples rather than a continuous power curve. A Riemann sum estimates the integral by calculating and adding the area between samples. More frequent, representative samples generally improve the estimate.

Create using interface (recommended)

  1. Go to helpers page

    Settings → Devices & services → Helpers tab → Create helper.

  2. Select Integral

    Search for integ if needed. A localized interface may use a translated label such as “Integral sensor.”

  3. Fill in the input sensor

    For "Input Sensor", select your power sensor whose unit is W. It is recommended that the name be something understandable, such as "Living room air conditioner electricity consumption."

  4. Set metric prefix and time unit

    These settings are critical. Set metric prefix to k and integration time to h. W × h ÷ 1000 = kWh. With prefix none, the result is Wh and its numeric value is 1000 times the kWh value.

  5. Leave the integration method as default

    The available methods are trapezoidal, left, and right. Trapezoidal is the default and is generally suitable when the source updates frequently. Change it only when the source’s reporting behavior requires another method.

  6. Set "maximum subinterval"

    Max sub-interval handles sources that do not report unchanged values. A value such as 60 seconds advances the integral even without a new sample; 0 disables the feature. Consider a value for loads that can remain at constant power for long periods.

Create with YAML

If you prefer to write files, the equivalent writing is:

sensor:
  - platform: integration
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_power
    name: Living-room AC energy
    unit_prefix: k
    unit_time: h
    method: trapezoidal
    max_sub_interval: "00:01:00"
    round: 3

source is the required source entity; unit_prefix and unit_time determine the output unit; method selects the integration method; round sets decimal places; and max_sub_interval limits time between integration updates.

Warning: An Integral sensor starts at 0 and cannot reconstruct earlier consumption. It can support trends from its creation time, but cannot reconcile a billing period that began earlier. For billing reconciliation, use a meter with an authoritative cumulative reading.
Tip: An Integral sensor has state_class set to total, not total_increasing. Both total and total_increasing are valid for Energy dashboard energy sources; do not change it merely because it is not total_increasing. It also derives device_class by changing the source class from power to energy.

Utility Meter: divide cumulative totals into billing cycles

An energy sensor may increase continuously from installation, while reports need totals for today, this month, or another cycle. The Utility Meter helper creates cycle-based totals that reset automatically.

Create using interface

  1. Open the helper again

    Settings → Devices & services → Helpers → Create helper → Utility Meter.

  2. Select input sensor

    Select the kWh sensor that was just accumulated (or the accumulated sensor that is already in the hardware).

  3. Select a reset cycle

    Meter reset period can be every 15 minutes, hourly, daily, weekly, monthly, bimonthly, quarterly, or yearly. Consider separate, clearly named daily, monthly, and yearly meters.

  4. Configure periodic resetting

    If the source can reset, such as after a smart plug loses power, keep Periodically resetting enabled so Utility Meter handles the decrease correctly. This option is enabled by default.

  5. Set offset if necessary

    "Meter reset offset (Offset)" can push the starting point of the cycle back. For example, if your electricity bill is settled on the 5th of every month, set the offset of the monthly cycle to 4 days to align the HA month with the bill.

YAML writing method

utility_meter:
  ac_daily:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living-room AC energy today
    cycle: daily
  ac_monthly:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living-room AC energy this month
    cycle: monthly
  ac_yearly:
    source: sensor.living_room_ac_energy
    name: Living-room AC energy this year
    cycle: yearly

Available configuration keys include source, name, unique_id, cycle, offset, cron, delta_values, net_consumption, tariffs, periodically_resetting, always_available. delta_values defaults to false(the source reports an absolute reading rather than increments),net_consumption defaults to false(only counting forward),periodically_resetting defaults to true.

Warning: The first Utility Meter cycle is incomplete. A weekly meter created at noon on Wednesday covers only part of that week. Exclude the first partial cycle from reports and begin comparisons with the next full cycle.

Utility Meter also generates actions

It provides two actions (Action, formerly known as Service):

action Purpose
utility_meter.calibrate Calibrate the meter to the specified value
utility_meter.reset reset the meter to zero

Utility Meter values are restored across restarts; restarting Home Assistant does not erase a monthly total.

Electricity prices and cost tracking

Multiply kWh by the applicable tariff to estimate cost. A statement such as “standby loads cost approximately [local-currency amount] per year” may be more actionable than “standby loads used 200 kWh,” provided that the tariff and calculation period are documented.

Three ways to count money

After adding a certain power source on the energy settings page, HA will ask you how to track costs. The options are roughly in these three categories:

way How it works Suitable for whom
Fixed unit price Enter one local-currency amount per kWh; Home Assistant applies it throughout. Most users who need an approximate cost
Entity for current unit price Select a sensor whose value is the current local-currency amount per kWh. Time-of-use or otherwise variable tariffs
Use one entity to represent the total amount You calculate the accumulated amount yourself and HA uses it directly. Advanced players who already have a ready amount of sensors
Note: This edition uses provider-neutral, local-currency examples. Check tariffs and availability for your utility and region. After you enable cost tracking, Home Assistant creates the corresponding cost statistic for that energy source and displays energy and cost together.

How regional electricity tariffs affect cost tracking

Many residential tariffs have two characteristics that make "a fixed unit price" inaccurate:

  • Tiered rates: Usage above a threshold may be charged at a higher unit price, increasing the average rate as consumption rises.
  • Seasonal rates: Some utilities charge different rates during defined seasons.
Warning: Rate tiers, seasonal definitions, taxes, and unit prices can change. This guide intentionally provides no tariff amounts. Use your utility’s current tariff or your latest bill, and enter values in the Home Assistant currency configured for your location.
Tip: For an estimate, divide the total charge on a recent bill by its billed kWh and use the result as an average local-currency unit price. Document what the bill total includes, because taxes and fixed charges may make this differ from the energy tariff. Update the estimate when rates or usage patterns change.

Configure a time-of-use tariff

For a time-of-use tariff, use the Utility Meter Tariffs Function:

utility_meter:
  home_daily:
    source: sensor.home_energy
    name: Household energy today
    cycle: daily
    tariffs:
      - peak
      - offpeak
  home_monthly:
    source: sensor.home_energy
    name: Household energy this month
    cycle: monthly
    tariffs:
      - peak
      - offpeak

This creates four sensors—sensor.home_daily_peak, sensor.home_daily_offpeak, sensor.home_monthly_peak, and sensor.home_monthly_offpeak—plus select.home_daily and select.home_monthly entities that select the active tariff.

Warning: The top-level key home_daily determines the entity ID; name affects only the display name. Use ASCII letters, digits, and underscores for keys, such as home_daily, so entity IDs remain easy to type and reference. See Chapter 4.

Then write an automation to change the select at the switching time point:

alias: Switch to the peak tariff
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "16:00:00"
conditions:
  - condition: time
    weekday:
      - mon
      - tue
      - wed
      - thu
      - fri
actions:
  - action: select.select_option
    target:
      entity_id: select.home_daily
    data:
      option: peak
mode: single

Create a corresponding automation to select offpeak. Use the exact times, weekdays, holidays, and seasonal rules in your utility tariff.

Warning: If several Utility Meters use tariffs, switch each select entity. Omitting one produces incorrect tariff totals for that meter. Target multiple select entities in one automation by listing them under entity_id.

Gas and water data

If you just want to save money on electricity bills, this is enough. But for a complete ESG or sustainability inventory, electricity is only one component, gas and water also count.

The Energy dashboard has separate gas and water sections. Each requires a cumulative sensor with device_class set to gas or water, state_class set to total_increasing or total, and a supported volume unit. You may also configure a local-currency unit price.

The challenge is hardware. Many residential gas and water meters provide no ready-made smart interface. Common external reading methods include:

practice Principle difficulty
Image recognition module Use a small lens to take a chart of the surface, and run recognition on the device to read out the numbers. , the lens and light source must be fixed
Magnetic sensor There is a rotating magnet inside the membrane gas meter, and a magnetometer is used to detect the number of rotations. Medium to high, you have to find the right position
Reed switch The magnet in the detection meter is triggered once every revolution, which is converted into dosage. , not every watch has this magnetic point
Radio reception (RTL-SDR) Some electricity, gas, and water meters regularly broadcast readings using wireless protocols, which can be received with a cheap USB TV stick. High, it depends on whether your local watch is broadcasting
Smart meter communication port (P1) Electric meters in some countries have standardized data output ports that can be read directly, and the gas consumption is also spit out. Low, but this is the specification in the Netherlands, Belgium, and Luxembourg. Availability elsewhere depends on the local meter standard.
Danger: Never open or modify a gas meter, touch its internal components, or create sparks nearby. Use only external, noncontact observation methods permitted by your utility and local rules. If uncertain, record the monthly reading manually instead.
Tip: If automatic reading is unavailable, create a Number helper and enter the meter reading each month. Twelve monthly points can support a basic annual trend, provided that dates, units, and reading methods remain consistent.

Turn energy data into an evidence-based report

A credible report needs both measured data and a comparison baseline. Use this structure to turn dashboard charts into a concise account of what changed.

Step 1: Establish a baseline (Baseline)

Before making changes, measure a complete baseline period: at least two weeks and preferably one month. Compare later results with the same duration and similar operating conditions.

Warning: Keep operating practices consistent during the baseline period. If you implement measures while collecting the baseline, you cannot attribute later differences reliably.

Step 2: Find the largest energy load

Start with three views:

Where to look What to look for common cause
Individual-device ranking Which device has the highest total kWh Air conditioner, dehumidifier, electric water heater
Electricity consumption in the early morning hours What is it that is still running when everyone is asleep? Water dispenser insulation, set-top box, NAS, old refrigerator
The shape of the daily curve Is there a platform that should be lowered or not? Dehumidifiers and devices with high standby power consumption that you forgot to turn off
Concept: Overnight demand, such as at 3:00 a.m., is a useful estimate of standby base load. Multiplying a stable kW demand by 24 hours and 30 days estimates its monthly kWh impact. Reducing persistent loads can offer a strong return.

Step Three: Common Office Improvement Measures

  • Pantry water heater schedule: use Chapter 8 Learn the automation so that the water heater is only powered on during working hours. The insulation electricity for two to three days on weekends and consecutive holidays is directly saved.
  • Automatic power off after work: Photocopiers, screens, and desktop devices are connected to smart sockets and turned off at 10 p.m.
  • Air conditioner setting temperature control: There is no need to actually change the air conditioner, just measure it first. By looking at "air conditioning electricity consumption" and "outdoor temperature" together, you can calculate how much you will save by increasing the temperature by one degree.
  • Abnormal power usage notification: Set up an automation that will push a notification when the total power during non-working hours exceeds a certain threshold (see how to do it) Chapter 7). This trick often catches "someone forgets to close".

Step 4: Write a report

A usable energy-saving report only needs these few paragraphs and does not need to be written like a paper:

  1. Measurement method

    Write clearly which circuits or devices you measured, what devices you used, and the date from which the data was measured. Be honest about the parts you didn't measure (e.g. "Lighting circuit not covered"), which actually adds credibility.

  2. Baseline data

    Monthly power consumption, daily average, and standby base load before improvement. Attached are the monthly review figures for the energy dashboard.

  3. Measures taken

    One column at a time, write "what you did" and "when you started" in each column. If there is automation, write down the trigger conditions. This is your execution evidence.

  4. Improved data and comparison

    Compare energy use over equal-length periods. Report both absolute change in kWh and percentage change, then add a local-currency estimate using the documented tariff.

  5. Honest labeling of distractions

    "This is a non-summer month, the temperature is low, and part of the drop is due to seasonal factors." - A report that writes this sentence is a hundred times more credible than a report that looks perfect.

Tip: To export raw numbers, the energy dashboard chart can be switched to different time ranges and read one by one, or the corresponding Utility Meter sensor can be queried on the History page. If you need to export in large quantities, the community has some custom components for statistics export, but that is beyond the scope of this chapter.

Advanced: device ranking and solar self-consumption

Power consumption ranking of individual devices

Add the kWh sensor of each socket to the "Individual Device" section of the energy settings, and the dashboard will automatically help you sort out which device uses the most energy.

If a main meter and its downstream devices are all listed, their values can be double-counted because the main meter already includes downstream consumption. Use Home Assistant’s upstream device setting to define the hierarchy.

Warning: To establish hierarchies, All related entities must be added to the "Individual Device" list first. Only devices that are already on the list will appear in the "Upstream Devices" drop-down menu. If you reverse the order, you won't be able to find the options.

Instantaneous power distribution

The newer Distribution card compares compatible entities with horizontal proportional bars. Its interactive legend recalculates percentages as you include or exclude items. Add power sensors to see current demand by circuit or appliance and identify the source of a sudden load increase.

Warning: The card requires compatible entity types and units. Do not mix W and kWh on one card; compare either power or energy.

The Energy dashboard’s Now view includes badges for current electric power, gas flow, and water flow. Water also has a Sankey chart. Use this view for a quick picture of current demand before building custom cards.

Self-consumption rate of solar energy

If you have solar energy, you will care about one indicator: how much of the generated electricity is used by yourself (self-use), and how much is sold back to the grid.

The energy dashboard itself, once the solar source is set up, will show the amount of electricity produced in relation to what goes in and out of the grid. If you want a clear percentage, you can use the template sensor to calculate it yourself - the concept is "(total power generation − feed power) ÷ total power generation":

template:
  - sensor:
      - name: "Solar self-consumption ratio"
        unique_id: solar_self_consumption_ratio
        unit_of_measurement: "%"
        state_class: measurement
        availability: >
          {{ is_number(states('sensor.solar_production_monthly'))
             and is_number(states('sensor.grid_export_monthly'))
             and states('sensor.solar_production_monthly') | float(0) > 0 }}
        state: >
          {% set gen = states('sensor.solar_production_monthly') | float(0) %}
          {% set exp = states('sensor.grid_export_monthly') | float(0) %}
          {{ ((gen - exp) / gen * 100) | round(1) }}

Replace sensor.solar_production_monthly and sensor.grid_export_monthly with your monthly Utility Meter sensors. The availability template suppresses calculation until both sources are numeric and generation is greater than zero.

Tip: Do not assign a device_class to this percentage; it is neither energy nor power. Use only unit_of_measurement: "%" and state_class: measurement.

How long should the data be retained?

To manage database growth, distinguish detailed Recorder history from long-term statistics:

Type What to save How long to keep
Recorder history A complete breakdown of every status change by purge_keep_days control, Default 10 days
Long-term statistics Aggregated statistical values (this is what the energy dashboard uses) It will not disappear with the general history clearing. You can view monthly reports from long ago.
Concept: History may retain only 10 days of detail while the Energy dashboard still shows the same month last year because it uses long-term statistics. Adjust Recorder retention only when you need more detailed recent data; increasing it does not improve long-term energy reports and enlarges the database.

Common problems

  1. My sensor cannot be selected in the drop-down menu of energy settings.

    Open Settings → Tools → States and verify device_class is energy (or gas/water), state_class is total_increasing or total, and unit_of_measurement is supported. Capitalization matters: kWh is valid; KWH and kwh are not. Also confirm that you selected the cumulative energy sensor rather than the instantaneous W sensor.

  2. One day, electricity consumption suddenly surged to several thousand kilowatt hours.

    Typical symptoms are that after the source sensor is reset to zero, it is regarded as "continuous accumulation", or state_class selected total But the device actually resets to zero, and HA counts "jumping from 0 back to a big number" as a huge increment.
    Treat the root cause: put the sensor state_class Change to total_increasing; If using Utility Meter, make sure "Periodic Zeroing" is turned on.
    Correct the bad statistic: Open Settings → Tools → Statistics, find the entity, use the date and time field to search for the exploded data point, and directly change the value back to a reasonable number. This page is where the Home Assistant UI fixes statistics. When there is an issue with the entity, a "Fix issue" link will appear next to it.

  3. Set it up but the dashboard remains blank

    First make sure you have waited long enough. The statistics are written in hourly units. Of course, they will be blank immediately after setting them. Please wait at least an hour or two. Then make sure there is indeed an "Energy" item in the sidebar - if not, check configuration.yaml of default_config: Has it been deleted? Finally, confirm whether the source sensor is moving now, if it is stuck unavailable or unknown, that's a device connection issue, not a dashboard issue.

  4. Repairs pops up "state class is incompatible with device class"

    The common invalid combination is device_class: power with total_increasing. Power is instantaneous and must use measurement. If the sensor is provided by an integration, go to the integration or device firmware report; if it is a template sensor you wrote yourself, directly change your YAML.

  5. The integrated number is 1000 times larger or 1000 times smaller.

    Metric prefix (unit_prefix) and time unit (unit_time) is not paired properly. Integrate from W to kWh, prefix to k, time required h. Leaving the prefix blank results in Wh. If you find a setting error, delete the helper and rebuild it. Changing the setting will not go back and correct the accumulated old data.

  6. The appliance is clearly running, but the sensor is not moving.

    The source power sensor may "not report if the value does not change". The points will only advance if there are new sampling points. Go to the settings of the integration helper and set the "Max sub-interval" to a value (for example, 60 seconds) to force it to advance by time.

  7. Utility Meter's first month numbers are weird

    This is expected behavior, and Home Assistant stated that the first cycle must be incomplete. Wait until the next full cycle starts to take a look. When making reports, discard the entire first period.

  8. The cost always shows 0

    Check whether you have actually selected the cost tracking method in the energy settings (the default is not to track). If "Use an entity to represent the current unit price" is selected, confirm whether the unit price sensor currently has a normal value. If it is unknown, multiplied out is naturally 0. In addition, cost statistics, like electricity consumption, will not appear for the first time until one hour.

FAQ

Do I have to buy hardware? Can I use an estimate?
You can make an estimate, but don’t use the estimate for external reporting. The method is to use Chapter 12 As mentioned, write a sample sensor: when a certain switch is on When outputting the nominal power of the appliance,off When outputting 0, points are taken. This is enough for "how much does this lamp probably eat", but it assumes that electrical appliances always operate at nominal power. The power of air conditioners and refrigerators will cause machine errors to be very large. If you want to convince people with numbers, you still have to really measure them.
The numbers on the energy dashboard don’t match the utility bill. Did I set it wrong?
A difference does not necessarily mean misconfiguration. Your sensors may cover only part of the site; a Riemann sum is an estimate affected by sampling; and Home Assistant’s calendar month may not match the utility’s meter-reading cycle. Align a Utility Meter with the billing cycle by using its offset.

Treat the dashboard as a trend tool unless you are using an authoritative whole-site meter and matching billing boundaries. Reconcile against the bill only after accounting for meter scope, tariff, taxes, and fixed charges.
Which one should be used for "Points" or "Utility Meter"? Do I need to build both?
They solve different problems, and you may need both.

Integral: converts instantaneous power in W into cumulative energy in kWh. Use it only when the device lacks a cumulative energy sensor.
Utility Meter: divides an existing cumulative kWh total into daily, monthly, or yearly cycles.The flow is

power → Integral → cumulative energy → Utility Meter → cycle totals. If the plug already reports cumulative kWh, skip Integral.
I have renamed the sensor or replaced it with a new socket. Will the previous historical data be lost?
Statistics are tied to the entity ID (entity_id). Only change display name Does not affect history. change Entity ID If so, HA will usually ask you if you want to move the statistics as well. If you select "Yes", it will be retained.

but if you are Replaced a physical device, that is, a brand new entity and a new count. The old data is still on the old entity, but the old and new data cannot be connected, and the chart will have breakpoints. So you have to think clearly about naming at the beginning - this is exactly what Chapter 4 Something I keep stressing. Before changing devices, copy the monthly statistics of the old data and back them up.
If I set up multiple sensors, will my SD card or hard drive be overwritten?
There will be an impact, but not as severe as imagined. Power sensors are the type with the largest amount of writes due to frequent changes. A few suggestions: First, the recorder (Recorder) details are preset to only stay for 10 days. This is already a very restrained setting, do not increase it casually; second, the long-term statistics are summarized and are much smaller in size, so don’t worry; third, if you really care, use the Recorder settings to exclude high-frequency sensors that you do not need to see the details, and the statistics will still be recorded as usual.

In addition, if you are still using an SD card to run HA and want to record energy data for a long time, it is recommended that you seriously consider switching to an SSD. SD card life is not friendly to frequent writing. Please read about backup Chapter 9.
I have no solar power or batteries and only a few sockets. Is this still useful?
Very useful, and that's where most people start. You can make valuable things with just three sockets: one for air conditioners, one for dehumidifiers, and one for old appliances you suspect. Run for a month and you will clearly see who the protagonist is.

The "Individual Devices" section of the energy dashboard is designed for this scenario, and you don't need a whole-house meter to use it. Once you are familiar with it and are sure that it is of value to you, it is not too late to do a whole-household measurement.
How long does it take to accumulate data before we can begin to draw conclusions?
It depends on what conclusion you want to draw.

"Which appliance consumes the most electricity?": You can get a rough idea in one week, and it’s quite reliable in two weeks.
"My energy-saving measures are effective": Improve at least the month before and the month after the improvement, and pay attention to seasonal interference - compare July with October, more than half of the decrease is due to the weather, not you. The ideal would be to compare a certain month this year with the same month last year, but that would have to wait a year.
"Annual total": To be honest, it takes a whole year. Collect the materials first in the first year, and then compare them in the second year.
