Chapter 22

Life logging and custom dashboards

Use helpers to record habits, household spending, meter readings, maintenance, and family notes that sensors cannot capture automatically. Then turn those records into useful charts and a dashboard you will actually use.

Why do you need to make your own records?

After installing a smart home, you will find one thing: temperature and humidity, battery level, and whether the door is opened or not, all have sensors to help you remember them. But the things we really want to track in life do not have sensors.

  • The air purifier filter on the balcony was last replaced in March or April? Can't remember.
  • How much does the total cost of water, electricity and gas cost this month? We didn't know until the bill came, it was too late.
  • How long has it been since the aquarium water was changed? Relying on memory means you may notice only after the water becomes cloudy.
  • Mom's message "Don't run the dishwasher today" was written on a note on the refrigerator, and you didn't even enter the kitchen.

What these things have in common is: The information exists only in memory, not in a system. As long as you plug it into Home Assistant, it can retain a timeline, draw charts, and send a reminder when it is due.

After this chapter you will have these additional abilities:

  1. Turn anything into a recordable entity

    Numbers, text, options, dates, times, each of the five helpers corresponds to a recording need, and you will know which one to use when.

  2. Let the record draw itself into a chart

    Know why some numbers can draw long-term trend charts, while others cannot, and how to remedy them.

  3. Process raw numbers into meaningful metrics

    Use Utility Meter to automatically settle monthly payments, and use Template sensors to calculate "how many days since the last maintenance".

  4. Create a really useful dashboard

    Sections layout, conditional display cards, phones and tablets see different things.

Concept: The basic creation method of helpers (Helper) is Appendix B As mentioned before, the editing, tab, and views types of the dashboard are Chapter 6. This chapter does not focus on the operating interface, but goes directly to "using them to create four types of real recording systems".

Do it yourself: Make "How many glasses of water did you drink today" in five minutes?

Start with a simple example: add a dashboard button that records each glass of water and resets the count at midnight.

  1. Create counter

    Go to Settings → Devices & Services → Helpers, press Create helper, choose Counter. Type "Number of glasses of water to drink today" in the name, and the icon can be filled in mdi:cup-water, fill in 0 for the minimum value, 1 for the step value, and leave the others as default. press Create.

  2. Confirm its entity ID

    Open the new helper and check its entity ID. A localized display name may produce an unclear generated ID, so change it to a readable value such as counter.water_cups_today. Chapter 4 explains naming rules and the effects of changing IDs.

  3. Put it on the dashboard and call out the plus and minus buttons

    Go back to your dashboard, top right corner Edit, press Add card, search for "Water consumed today" and select Tile cards. Here’s where everyone gets stuck most often: By default, the tile card will only display a number and will not have add or subtract buttons.. Scroll down on the card editing screen and find Features, press Add, select Counter actions, check which numbers to display (increment, decrement, reset to zero). The button will not appear on the card until it is saved.

  4. Make an automation that automatically returns to zero in the middle of the night

    Go to Settings → Automations & Scenes & Scenes → Create Automation, switch to YAML mode and paste the following paragraph. If you are not familiar with the automated three-stage structure, look back Chapter 8.

  5. Test

    On the automation editing screen, press Run, see if the counter reaches 0. If there is, it means the entire road is open.

alias: Reset daily water count at midnight
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "00:00:00"
conditions: []
actions:
  - action: counter.reset
    target:
      entity_id: counter.water_cups_today
mode: single

If you switch to YAML mode and look at that tile, it looks like this. If you want to make the second and third counters in the future, just copy and change the entities:

type: tile
entity: counter.water_cups_today
name: Water today
features:
  - type: counter-actions
    actions:
      - increment
      - decrement
      - reset
Tip: Digital helpers also have corresponding functions that can be added: function selection of tile cards Numeric input (type: numeric-input), style Can choose slider(slider) or buttons(plus and minus buttons). For things like weight and value that need to be typed, using this method is much faster than clicking into the dialog every time.
Tip: If you want to "save yesterday's numbers before returning to zero", in counter.reset Add one in front input_number.set_value, just write yesterday's value into another digital helper called "Number of glasses of water drank yesterday".

What does it look like on the dashboard?

It is recommended to put three things into one block: two tile cards (drinking water, exercise) and a gauge (Gauge) card to see the progress of drinking water today. The setting of the gauge card is very intuitive;min Default 0,max The default is 100, remember to adjust it according to the actual situation.

type: gauge
entity: counter.water_cups_today
name: Water today
unit: glasses
min: 0
max: 8
needle: true
segments:
  - from: 0
    color: var(--error-color)
  - from: 3
    color: var(--warning-color)
  - from: 6

Warning:segments(segmented discoloration) only if needle: true will take effect. If the pointer is turned off, the color will not be displayed even if it is set. This is where many people think they made a mistake.
Concept: The 8 cups above are just used as a scale for the progress bar. You can set it to 5 or 10. It is purely a visual reference line and has nothing to do with any recommended intake.

Want to see long-term trends? See the next step first

Now you may be thinking, "Then I want to see the chart of changes in weight over the past three months." Here is a pitfall that many people get stuck in: The helper does not produce long-term statistics, so take input_number.weight When trying to put the statistics-graph card, it will show that the statistics cannot be found. The solution is in the section "Processing Numbers" at the end, so remember to read on.

Example 2: Household income and expenditure and utility bill records

A. Write down an expense at random

Use two Number helpers and one script: one helper stores the new entry, the other stores the monthly total, and the script adds the entry to the total.

  1. Create three digital helpers

    input_number.expense_entry(Single value, minimum 0, maximum 100000, step 1, mode selection input box),input_number.expense_month_total(Cumulative this month, same range, fill in the unit local currency), plus one more input_number.expense_last_month(The setting for last month’s expenditure is the same as the accumulated one). The third one is not used now. It is used for monthly settlement below. Build it together to save the need to come back later.

  2. Create a script to add the value

    Go to Settings → Automations & Scenes & Scenes → Scripts, create a new script, cut the YAML pattern and paste the following content. Details of the script (fields, execution mode) are in Chapter 10.

  3. Add the script to a card

    Add an Entities card to the dashboard and place both Number helpers and the script on it. Enter a value, run the script, confirm that the total increases, and check that the entry resets.

  4. Automatic settlement on the first of every month

    The automation of settlement is as follows. It will first copy this month's total to "last month's expenses", and then reset the total to zero.

alias: Record an expense
sequence:
  - action: input_number.set_value
    target:
      entity_id: input_number.expense_month_total
    data:
      value: >-
        {{ states('input_number.expense_month_total') | float(0)
           + states('input_number.expense_entry') | float(0) }}
  - action: input_number.set_value
    target:
      entity_id: input_number.expense_entry
    data:
      value: 0
mode: single
alias: Close out expenses on the first of each month
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "00:05:00"
conditions:
  - condition: template
    value_template: "{{ now().day == 1 }}"
actions:
  - action: input_number.set_value
    target:
      entity_id: input_number.expense_last_month
    data:
      value: "{{ states('input_number.expense_month_total') | float(0) }}"
  - action: input_number.set_value
    target:
      entity_id: input_number.expense_month_total
    data:
      value: 0
mode: single
Tip: If you want to classify accounting (dining/transportation/entertainment), add a drop-down helper as a classification, and then use it in the script choose Determine which cumulative number to add to. Too many categories will be difficult to maintain. It is recommended to have up to four or five categories.

B. Hand-written water, electricity and gas degrees

Many water, electricity, and gas providers still read meters or bills manually. The method is to read the meter every time Cumulative degree Fill in a numeric helper and let Home Assistant figure out how much is used in each issue.

ComponentPurposeSettings
input_number.meter_electricityThe current accumulated degree of the electric meterSet the maximum value larger (for example, 999999), step 1, unit kWh
input_number.meter_waterThe current accumulated degree of the water meterunit m³
input_datetime.meter_read_dateThe date of this meter readingOnly check "Date"
Warning: Cumulative degree Can only be added upwards, do not fill in “How many times did you use this issue” in each issue? Only by filling in the cumulative value can Home Assistant help you automatically settle the installments; this is also the prerequisite for the Utility Meter to work later.

If your home already has a smart meter, power sensor, or smart socket with power reading function, it will be easier to skip the manual copy and feed the sensor directly to Utility Meter.

Example 3: Device maintenance records and automatic reminders

These requirements all have the same shape: Remember the last date → Calculate how many days have passed → Remind if the cycle is exceeded. Once you learn one, you can use it for filtering, changing water, cleaning air conditioners, and replacing toothbrush heads.

  1. Create a date helper component

    Go to Settings → Devices & Services → Helpers → Create helper → Date/Time. Name it “Filter last changed,” select only Date, and leave Time clear. The suggested entity ID is input_datetime.filter_last_change.

  2. Build a template sensor that counts days

    Go to Settings → Devices & Services → Helpers, select Create helper, choose Template, then Sensor. Name it “Days since filter change,” paste the template below into the state template, and set the unit of measurement to days. After creating it, change the entity ID to sensor.filter_days_since for the later automation.

  3. Create a “Changed today” button

    Create a script that sets the date to today. In this way, you only need to click once to change the filter, and you do not have to manually select the calendar.

  4. Automate reminders

    It will be checked at 9 a.m. every day and will be pushed to the phone after 90 days. See push settings Chapter 7.

{{ ((now().timestamp()
     - as_timestamp(states('input_datetime.filter_last_change'), 0))
     / 86400) | int(0) }}
Concept: | int(0) discards the fractional part and counts complete days. A filter changed this morning therefore remains at 0 days until a full day has passed. Using round(0) could show 1 day after only half a day. The 0 in parentheses is the fallback when no date is set or the entity is temporarily unavailable, preventing an unknown result.
alias: The filter has been replaced
sequence:
  - action: input_datetime.set_datetime
    target:
      entity_id: input_datetime.filter_last_change
    data:
      date: "{{ now().strftime('%Y-%m-%d') }}"
mode: single
alias: Filter replacement reminder
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "09:00:00"
conditions:
  - condition: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.filter_days_since
    above: 89
actions:
  - action: notify.persistent_notification
    data:
      title: Living-room air purifier
      message: >-
        The filter has been in use for {{ states('sensor.filter_days_since') }} days. It is time to replace it.
mode: single
Tip: If you want to manage several devices at the same time, don't duplicate four copies of automation. Use a drop-down helper to list all devices with a drop-down card to switch, or create a "date + day sensor" for each device, and then use multiple numeric_state conditions. The second approach is more work but easier to understand. It is recommended to use the second one at first.

If you want to see all the maintenance status at a glance on the dashboard, it is easiest to use Markdown cards. content You can write templates directly in the field:

type: markdown
title: Maintenance status
content: |-
  | Item | Elapsed | Interval |
  |---|---|---|
  | Air-purifier filter | {{ states('sensor.filter_days_since') }} days | 90 days |
  | Aquarium water change | {{ states('sensor.tank_days_since') }} days | 14 days |
  | AC cleaning | {{ states('sensor.ac_days_since') }} days | 180 days |

Example 4: Random notes and family message board

Text helpers for short messages

A Text helper is enough for a short message such as “Do not run the dishwasher today.” Its maximum length defaults to 100 and can be increased to 255, the hard limit for a Home Assistant entity state. Add the helper to an Entities card so you can edit it directly from the dashboard.

Add a date and time helper to record "who left it when", and the automation is like this:

alias: Record when the message changes
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: input_text.family_message
conditions: []
actions:
  - action: input_datetime.set_datetime
    target:
      entity_id: input_datetime.family_message_time
    data:
      datetime: "{{ now().strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S') }}"
mode: single

For list-like things, use to-do lists instead of words.

Shopping lists, things to fix, to-do lists, lists that are constantly growing and subtracting, can be difficult to use if shoehorned into a text field (and 255 characters quickly fills up). Home Assistant has built-in Local To-do Integration, the data is stored in your own home, no cloud account is required.

  1. Join the integration

    Settings → Devices & Services → Add integration, search Local to-do, follow the on-screen instructions to create a list, such as "Shopping list".

  2. Build a few more copies

    Multiple lists can be added repeatedly to the same integration. It is recommended to divide it into at least two parts: a "purchasing list" and a "home repairs to be made".

  3. Put on the dashboard

    Edit Dashboard →Add card→ Find To-do list Card, select the list you just created. You can check boxes and add new items directly on your phone.

  4. Let automation help you add items

    use todo.add_item This action. For example, the above filter reminder, in addition to promoting it, also adds "buy filter" to the purchase list.

actions:
  - action: todo.add_item
    target:
      entity_id: todo.shopping_list
    data:
      item: Buy a purifier filter

Other available actions are todo.update_item(Change content or mark complete),todo.remove_item(delete one item),todo.get_items(Read out the list),todo.remove_completed_items(Clear the ticks).

Tip: For a rotation of "Who takes out the trash today?" use a drop-down helper to list the names of family members, and then make an automatic daily input_select.select_next With automation, it becomes an automatic shift schedule.

Statistical charts: look at the long term, but there are prerequisites

This distinction determines whether an entity can appear in a long-term statistics graph.

Warning: Statistics graph cards only have long term statistics entity. Long-term statistics will only be generated for sensors with state class (State class), that is measurement, total, total_increasing One of these three. Helper (input_number, counter etc.) does not have a state class, so putting it in directly will show "statistics unavailable".

Use a template sensor to expose the helper’s value with a state class. Although you can create template sensors from the Helpers page, Home Assistant documentation says that the UI offers “a relatively streamlined subset of options” and may not expose every advanced field. The examples here therefore use configuration.yaml for more consistent access to those fields:

template:
  - sensor:
      - name: "Weight record"
        unique_id: weight_record
        unit_of_measurement: "kg"
        device_class: weight
        state_class: measurement
        state: "{{ states('input_number.weight') | float(0) }}"
        availability: "{{ is_number(states('input_number.weight')) }}"

Archived to the sidebar Tools → YAML tab, click "Template Entity" to reload, and then it will appear sensor.weight_record. Starting from version 2026.8, this menu has been officially renamed from "Developer tools" to "Tools", older versions are still called Developer Tools, and the location is the same. Put the new sensor into the statistics chart card:

type: statistics-graph
title: Weight trend
days_to_show: 90
chart_type: line
period: day
stat_types:
  - mean
entities:
  - entity: sensor.weight_record
    name: Weight
Concept: Qualified sensors will be recorded in two statistics: Short term statistics every 5 minutes, and compress it into Long-term statistics of one aggregate per hour (Save min/max/average). The short-term copy will be cleared regularly like the detailed records, and the long-term official documentation clearly states that will not be cleared, will be kept forever, so it can be stored for several years without the storage growing without limit. The cost is that the resolution is coarser than the original record.stat_types Optional min, max, mean, sum, state, change; period Optional 5minute, hour, day, week, month, year or auto.
Tip: For the newly built template sensor, it is normal for the statistics graph to be empty. Use period: day/week/month In this long period, it reads long-term statistics that are generated only once every hour, so the first point usually does not appear until the next hour. If you want to see something immediately, first use a history graph card, or put period Set to 5minute Look at short-term statistics.

Process raw numbers into meaningful metrics

Raw numbers (accumulated degrees, a weight value) are often difficult to read. Home Assistant has several ready-made helpers that can help you do calculations without having to write programs yourself.

Utility Meter: Automatic monthly settlement

it needs a A sensor that keeps adding up When the source. If you are taking the reading by hand, first use the template sensor to read it. input_number Wrap into one total_increasing of sensors:

template:
  - sensor:
      - name: "Cumulative meter reading"
        unique_id: meter_electricity_total
        unit_of_measurement: "kWh"
        device_class: energy
        state_class: total_increasing

Then come Settings → Devices & Services → Helpers → Create helper → Utility Meter, set the focus:

FieldWhat to fill in

As for the bar graph of "how many degrees are used each month", the most direct way is not to draw the monthly sensor, but to draw Source sensor that is always accumulating Throw it to the statistics graph card and let it calculate the monthly changes by itself (change):

  - change

Statistics helper: average

Create a location in the helpers, select Statistics. It will ask you which characteristic value (characteristic) you want. For numerical sensors, you can choose mean, median, sum, change, number of times, maximum value, minimum value, etc.

Warning: Sampling size and maximum age At least one must be set, the sensor will not move if both are empty. If you want the "average of the past 30 days", set the maximum time limit to 30 days.

Customizable dashboard: arrange records how you want to see them

Now that we have all the information, the next step is layout. The new dashboard view default is Sections Layout, this is also the best arrangement method currently.

What do you think about Sections layout?

Think of it like a bulletin board: the entire view is a grid on which you paste "blocks" and place cards in each block. Compared with the old Masonry layout, the biggest difference is You no longer have to rely on vertical-stack cards to force layout, the block itself is the grouping unit.

SettingsWhereWhat to do
Max columnsView settingsMaximum number of columns for wide screen. Suppose 3 to 4 are usually just right
Dense section placementView settingsAutomatically fill holes, the screen will be fuller but you will lose some arrangement control
Heading cardtop of each blockBlock title, optional heading_style for title or subtitle, and you can also hang a badge
BadgesView top / title cardThe small one shows the value of an entity, which is very well suited to displaying "Drink 5 glasses of water today"

    Write "This Month" on the title card, and put the physical card for this month's expenses, the accounting script button, and the monthly electricity consumption statistics chart card.

  4. The third block "Maintenance and To-Do"

  5. The fourth block "Trend"

    Put weight on chart cards with various long-term statistics. Normally you do not need to look at this area, put it at the bottom.

    entity: counter.workout_count

Conditional display cards: do not take up space if you shouldn’t see it

Conditional cards can cause the entire card to disappear when the conditions are not met. The types of conditions currently supported include state, numeric_state, screen, user, location, time, and and, or, not Can be combined.

type: conditional
conditions:
  - condition: numeric_state
    entity: sensor.filter_days_since
    above: 89
card:
  type: markdown
  content: The filter is more than 90 days old. Remember to replace it.

Phones and tablets see different things

screen The condition accepts CSS media-query strings, which can be used to create "detailed charts only displayed on large screens" and "lite versions only displayed on small screens".

type: conditional
conditions:
  - condition: screen
    media_query: "(min-width: 1024px)"
card:
  type: statistics-graph
  title: Full trend
  days_to_show: 365
  chart_type: line
  entities:
    - sensor.weight_record
type: conditional
conditions:
  - condition: screen
    media_query: "(max-width: 1023px)"
card:
  type: gauge
  entity: counter.water_cups_today
  name: Water today
  min: 0
  max: 8
Tip: Another simpler approach is Make two dashboards: One "mobile version" only contains the five or six most frequently clicked cards, and one "tablet version" contains all of them. The sidebar of the mobile app can hide unnecessary dashboards, which is much easier to maintain than multiple condition cards.

Still available user Conditions allow each family member to see their own record card. The user account is created in Chapter 5.

Export and save records

After accumulating records for a long time, you may want to take them out and calculate them yourself, or you may be afraid that one day the database will be broken. There are three levels:

Level 1: Download CSV directly

  1. Open history panel

    Enter from the sidebar History panel. This is the place to "draw numerical graphs and download numerical values". The other sidebar Activity (Activity, Logbook) The panel is a running account, which records "who opened what at what time." Starting from 2026.8, it also has its own CSV download, but if you want to export numbers, please go to the history panel.

  2. Select entity and time range

    Select the area, device or entity you want, and then set the time range.

  3. Click to download information

    There is in the upper-right corner Download data, will give you a CSV that can be dropped directly into your spreadsheet.

Warning: The Recorder default only retains 10 days Details of (purge_keep_days The default value is 10). For periods exceeding 10 days, the screen and downloads will automatically switch to long-term statistics, which are averaged over hourly sampling, and the values ​​will not be exactly the same as the original data. This is not a bug, it is a space-saving design.

Level 2: Let the backup take the database away with you

The default database for Home Assistant is in the settings directory home-assistant_v2.db, the built-in backup will cover it. The backup settings and restore process are in Chapter 9, if you are serious about accounting or record maintenance, please be sure to turn on the automatic backup schedule.

Level 3: Keep another copy of the important numbers yourself

The safest way: Automate your monthly settlement by writing the numbers into your to-do list, sending yourself a notification, or pushing a message with a summary of the month. In this way, even if the entire database fails, you will still have at least a written record.

actions:
  - action: todo.add_item
    target:
      entity_id: todo.household_log
    data:
      item: >-
        {{ now().strftime('%Y-%m') }} spending:
        {{ states('input_number.expense_month_total') | int(0) }} in local currency
Tip: What is cleared is detailed status records and short-term statistics every 5 minutes; Hourly long-term statistics will not be cleared, will always remain. Therefore, "detailed data only lasts for 10 days" does not mean that your long-term trend chart only lasts for 10 days. Charts such as weight and monthly electricity consumption will still be there after several years. To retain longer original Only the data needed to adjust the recorder purge_keep_days, the price is that the database becomes larger, so be careful with SD cards or small-capacity hard drives.

Troubleshooting

  1. Helper resets to zero after rebooting

    Under normal circumstances, numbers, text, drop-downs, date and time helpers will be restored to their values before reopening, and counters will also be restored. restore option and is on by default. It will be reset to zero usually because you filled it in when setting it using YAML. initial, this value has a higher priority than restoration, and your records will be overwritten when you start the computer. put initial Just take it off. Another possibility is that Home Assistant did not shut down properly (unplugged the power directly) and did not have time to write the status.

  2. The statistics graph card says there is no data

    Ninety percent of the time it's because you threw the helpers directly into it. Helpers have no state class (State class) and will not generate long-term statistics. Follow the section on "Growing Records into Charts" to pack a layer of template sensors and add state_class: measurement. Do not worry if the display is still empty after wrapping it up: the long-term statistics will not generate the first stroke until after the hour, so wait until the next hour before looking.

  3. The history chart is empty or has only one straight line

    First make sure Recorder has not excluded the entity. A value that never changes correctly appears as a straight line. If the History range exceeds 10 days, the panel switches to long-term statistics; a helper without long-term statistics then appears blank. To inspect the helper’s raw state changes, keep the range within 10 days and use a suitable hours_to_show value on the card.

  4. Cards are cut off or squished on your phone

    First confirm whether the view type is Sections. Check again to see if you are cramming too many items into the same stacked card. If the content of the Markdown card is very long, add card_size Let it reserve height. Table-type Markdown content is inherently prone to overflowing on narrow screens. For mobile versions, it is recommended to use columns instead of tables.

  5. The template sensor is built but the entity cannot be found.

    Changed configuration.yaml Be sure to reload. to the sidebar Tools (Before 2026.8, it was called “Developer Tools”)→ YAML tab and click "Template Entity" to reload. If it still does not work after reloading, it is probably because the YAML indentation is wrong. First use the “Check Settings” on the same page to check. There is also a common low-level error:configuration.yaml There is already one in template: block, and you posted a second one, YAML will only recognize the last one, and the sensors must be merged under the same block.

  6. Condition cards never show up

    The condition card is displayed by default when "all conditions must be met". use numeric_state Pay attention when above is strictly greater than, write above: 90 It will not trigger in exactly 90 days, you have to write above: 89. use screen When conditional, remember to enclose the entire media query string in quotation marks.

  7. The script was clicked but the accumulation did not change.

    Check input_number Is the maximum value exceeded by the result calculated by the template? If it exceeds the upper limit, it will be blocked. For additional use | float(0) This way of writing with preset values prevents entities from temporarily unavailable The entire template exploded.

FAQ

I still can't tell whether I should record using counters or numbers.
It depends on what your "input action" is. Click to add one, use the counter (counter), it has ready-made counter.increment and counter.reset. To fill in a specific value (62.4 kg, a value of 1284), use numbers (input_number), it is easier to type by setting the display mode to an input box. If you really hesitate, use a counter first, because it has one button to reset to zero, making daily records much more convenient.
I want to remember "an entire paragraph" of text, such as a shopping list, but the text Helper is too short
Don't fight the length limit. The maximum length of text helpers is defaulted to 100 and can only be adjusted up to 255 because the entity state itself is stuck at 255 characters. For list-like things, please use the built-in one. Local To-do Integration plus to-do list cards, data is stored locally, items can be added or deleted unlimitedly, and automation can also be used todo.add_item Just add stuff in.
I only want to look at the weight chart at one point per week, not up and down every day.
Two approaches. First, the statistics graph card period Set to week, stat_types Set to mean, it will help you calculate the weekly average. Second, create a Statistics helper, select the mean value (mean) for the characteristic value, set the maximum age (max age) to 7 days, and obtain a "last seven-day average" sensor before drawing the graph. The first is easier.
Does the source sensor of Utility Meter have to be a smart meter?
No, but the source must be one Sensor entities that accumulate upwards, cannot be fed directly input_number. If you want to write down the degree by hand, first write a sample sensor input_number Packaged state_class: total_increasing sensor, and then use that sensor as the input of the Utility Meter. Remember to set "Periodically resetting" when setting turn off—It is turned on by default, but real water and electricity meters will not reset themselves to zero. If you forget to turn it off, the consumption will be miscalculated.
I put the record card on my phone, but every time I have to scroll for a long time to find it
Make a special phone dashboard, only put the five or six cards that you actually press every day (drink water, exercise, billing buttons, leave messages), and put everything else on another tablet/computer dashboard. This is better than using multiple screen It is easy to maintain too much if the condition card is used to hide things. The sidebar of the mobile app can hide rarely used dashboards.
Will these records be transferred to the cloud? Is your family’s information safe?
Helpers, local to-do lists, and Recorder databases are all stored on your own Home Assistant host and will not be transferred externally by default. There are two things to note: First, if you enable external access, the dashboard can be seen from the outside. Please see the permissions and account settings. Chapter 5 Second, the backup file contains these records. You need to think clearly about where to put the backup file. The details are in Chapter 9.
The numbers shown on the chart are different from my own calculations
Chances are you're looking at long-term statistics rather than raw records. Data that exceeds the recorder retention period (default 10 days) will automatically switch to long-term statistics, which are concentrated values ​​sampled every hour and averaged. It is normal to be different from each raw number. To check the exact numbers, narrow the time range to less than 10 days, or just download the CSV and see for yourself.
Do I have to install HACS community cards to create a good-looking dashboard?
No. The Sections layout plus tile cards, title cards, badges, statistics graph cards, and Markdown cards can already create a fairly complete record dashboard. The value of community cards is to fill specific needs that the built-in ones can't do (such as complex charts in ApexCharts), but every additional one you install is another feature that may break when upgrading. It is recommended to use the built-in cards to the limit first, then install them if they are really stuck, and only install the ones that are still being updated.
